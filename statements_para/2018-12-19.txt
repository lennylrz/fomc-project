The Committee has opted to increase the target range for the federal funds rate by a quarter of a percentage point.

Recent data indicates continued strengthening in the labor market and robust economic expansion. Employment growth has been solid over recent months, and the unemployment rate remains low. Consumer spending continues to rise significantly, though the pace of business fixed investment has slowed from earlier in the year. Both headline inflation and core inflation, excluding food and energy, are currently close to 2 percent over the past year. Measures of long-term inflation expectations show little overall change.

The Committee's mandate is to promote maximum employment and price stability. It believes that additional, gradual increases in the policy rate will support sustained economic growth, a strong labor market, and inflation near its symmetric 2 percent target over the medium term. The Committee assesses that the risks to the economic outlook are generally balanced, but it will continue to observe global economic and financial developments and their potential impact.

Future adjustments to the policy rate's timing and magnitude will depend on the Committee's evaluation of current and projected economic conditions against its goals of maximum employment and symmetric 2 percent inflation. This evaluation will incorporate various data, including labor market indicators, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments.

Today, the Committee chose to decrease its primary interest rate by half a percentage point. Concurrently, the Board of Governors sanctioned a similar reduction in the rate at which financial institutions can borrow from the central bank.

Confidence among consumers and businesses has continued to decline, worsened by increasing energy expenses that diminish household buying power and squeeze corporate earnings. This situation has contributed to a notable decline in retail transactions and corporate investment in equipment. Consequently, factory output has been significantly curtailed, with new technologies seemingly hastening the adjustment of production and demand to potential surpluses in inventories and capital goods.

Considering these factors, and with price increases under control, a swift and strong monetary policy adjustment was deemed necessary. Nevertheless, enduring technological progress and associated productivity improvements show little sign of slowing, and these benefits, alongside lower borrowing costs, should foster economic expansion over time.

Despite this, the Committee maintains its view that, given its long-term objectives of stable prices and sustained economic expansion, and based on current information, the predominant risks lean towards conditions that could lead to economic softness in the near future.

In approving the discount rate adjustment, the Federal Reserve Board endorsed proposals from the Boards of Directors of the Federal Reserve Banks of New York, Philadelphia, Cleveland, Atlanta, Chicago, St. Louis, Minneapolis, Dallas, and San Francisco.

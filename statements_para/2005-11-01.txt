The Committee has opted to increase the federal funds rate target by a quarter of a percentage point.

Current economic output and job figures have been negatively impacted by high energy costs and disruptions from recent hurricanes. Nevertheless, supportive monetary policy, alongside strong fundamental productivity growth, continues to bolster economic activity. This support is anticipated to be further enhanced by reconstruction efforts in the hurricane-affected regions. While the accumulated increases in energy and other expenses could contribute to inflationary pressures, core inflation has been modest recently, and long-term inflation expectations remain stable.

The Committee believes that, with suitable monetary policy adjustments, the potential for both stronger and weaker outcomes for sustained growth and price stability should be balanced. Given that underlying inflation is expected to remain in check, the Committee judges that the removal of monetary accommodation can proceed at a gradual pace. However, the Committee stands ready to adjust its approach in response to evolving economic conditions to uphold its commitment to price stability.

Separately, the Board of Governors unanimously endorsed a 25-basis point rise in the discount rate. This decision followed requests from the Boards of Directors of numerous Federal Reserve Banks.

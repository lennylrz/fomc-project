The Committee has opted to decrease its primary policy interest rate by half a percentage point. Concurrently, the Board of Governors sanctioned a similar half-percentage-point reduction in the discount rate.

Regarding the economic landscape, substantial progress has been made in reducing surplus stockpiles. Consumer spending and residential investment have shown resilience, though their recent momentum has plateaued. Conversely, business investment in productive assets continues its downward trend. The ongoing decline in corporate earnings, both current and projected, coupled with considerable uncertainty regarding future economic conditions, is anticipated to suppress capital expenditures. This potential constraint, alongside the possible impact of past declines in household wealth on consumption and the threat of decelerated international growth, continues to exert downward pressure on the economy.

With diminishing pressures in both employment and goods markets, price increases are expected to remain subdued. While productivity growth as measured paused in the first three months of the year, the robust underlying pace observed in recent years appears largely preserved, bolstering long-term prospects.

The Committee maintains its view that, considering its objectives of stable prices and sustained economic expansion, and based on current data, the predominant risks lean towards conditions that could lead to economic fragility in the near term.

In approving the discount rate adjustment, the Federal Reserve Board endorsed proposals from the Boards of Directors of the Federal Reserve Banks of New York, Richmond, Chicago, St. Louis, and San Francisco.

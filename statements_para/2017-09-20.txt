The Committee chose to keep its policy interest rate unchanged at this meeting. This decision reflects an accommodative monetary policy stance, which supports further labor market improvement and sustained progress toward the 2 percent inflation target.

Recent data since [month] indicate continued strengthening in the job market and moderate economic expansion this year. Employment gains have been consistent, and the unemployment rate remains low. Household spending has grown moderately, and business fixed investment has increased in recent quarters. Over the past year, both overall inflation and core inflation have decreased and are currently below 2 percent. Market-based inflation compensation measures are still low, while survey-based long-term inflation expectations are largely stable.

The Committee's dual mandate is to achieve maximum employment and price stability. While recent severe weather events have caused significant hardship and will temporarily affect economic activity, they are not expected to alter the national economic trajectory over the medium term. Therefore, the Committee anticipates moderate economic growth and further labor market strengthening with gradual policy adjustments. Although higher gasoline and other prices due to the storms will likely temporarily boost inflation, inflation is expected to remain somewhat below 2 percent in the near term before stabilizing around the 2 percent objective over the medium term. The near-term economic risks appear generally balanced, but the Committee is closely watching inflation trends.

Future adjustments to the policy rate will depend on how economic conditions, both realized and projected, align with the Committee's goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. This assessment will consider various factors, including labor market indicators, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments. The Committee will closely monitor actual and expected inflation relative to its symmetric inflation target. The Committee expects that economic conditions will likely warrant gradual increases in the policy rate, which is anticipated to remain below its long-run equilibrium for some time. However, the actual path of the policy rate will be guided by incoming economic data.

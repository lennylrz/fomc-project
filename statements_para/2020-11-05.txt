The Committee has decided to maintain its current policy rate.

Economic activity and employment continue to recover, though they remain significantly below pre-[event] levels. Consumer price inflation is being held down by subdued demand and earlier declines in oil prices. Financial conditions remain supportive, partly due to policy actions aimed at bolstering the economy and credit availability for households and businesses.

The trajectory of the economy is heavily dependent on the progression of the virus. The ongoing public health crisis will continue to suppress economic activity, employment, and inflation in the near term, and presents substantial risks to the medium-term economic outlook.

The Committee aims for maximum employment and inflation at a 2 percent rate over the long run. Given that inflation has consistently been below this long-term objective, the Committee intends to allow inflation to moderately exceed 2 percent for a period, ensuring that inflation averages 2 percent over time and that long-term inflation expectations remain firmly anchored at 2 percent. The Committee anticipates maintaining an accommodative monetary policy stance until these objectives are met. The Committee expects to keep the policy rate at its current level until labor market conditions align with its assessment of maximum employment and inflation reaches 2 percent and is on track to moderately surpass 2 percent for some time. Furthermore, over the coming months, the Federal Reserve will continue to expand its holdings of Treasury and agency mortgage-backed securities at least at the current pace to ensure smooth market operations and foster accommodative financial conditions, thereby facilitating credit flow to households and businesses.

In evaluating the appropriate monetary policy stance, the Committee will continue to observe incoming information and its implications for the economic outlook. The Committee stands ready to adjust policy as necessary should risks arise that could hinder the achievement of its goals. These assessments will consider a broad range of data, including public health developments, labor market conditions, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments.

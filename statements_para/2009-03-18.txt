The Committee decided to keep its policy interest rate unchanged.

Recent data indicates a continued economic contraction. Consumer confidence and spending have been negatively impacted by job losses, reduced equity and housing values, and restricted credit availability. Businesses have scaled back inventories and fixed investments due to weaker sales forecasts and challenges in securing credit. American exports have declined as several key trading partners are also experiencing recessions.

Despite a weak short-term outlook, the Committee anticipates that measures to stabilize financial markets and institutions, alongside fiscal and monetary stimulus, will gradually restore sustainable economic growth. Given the growing economic underutilization both domestically and internationally, the Committee expects inflation to remain low. Furthermore, there is a risk that inflation could stay below levels conducive to long-term economic growth and price stability for some time.

Under these conditions, the Federal Reserve will utilize all available instruments to foster economic recovery and maintain price stability. The Committee expects that economic circumstances will necessitate exceptionally low levels of the policy rate for an extended duration. To bolster mortgage lending and housing markets, the Committee opted to expand the Federal Reserve's balance sheet further by acquiring additional agency mortgage-backed securities and increasing its purchases of agency debt this year. Additionally, to improve private credit market conditions, the Committee chose to acquire longer-term Treasury securities over the next six months. The Federal Reserve has initiated a facility to facilitate credit extension to households and small businesses, and the range of eligible collateral for this facility is likely to be broadened. The Committee will continue to closely observe the scale and composition of the Federal Reserve's balance sheet as financial and economic conditions evolve.

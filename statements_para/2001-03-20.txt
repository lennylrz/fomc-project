The Committee chose to decrease its primary interest rate by half a percentage point. Concurrently, the Board of Governors sanctioned a similar half-percentage-point reduction in the rate at which financial institutions borrow from the central bank.

Businesses are facing ongoing pressure on their earnings, which is hindering investment. The decline in stock market values is also impacting household spending. The accumulation of unsold goods has led to a swift reduction in factory output. However, with a slight improvement in overall spending since last year, it seems that businesses are making progress in adjusting their stock levels.

While current economic trends do not seem to have significantly harmed the outlook for long-term productivity gains, the economy has recently developed more capacity than it is currently using. The potential for this underutilization to persist and the risk of a downturn in the global economy suggest a significant chance that demand and production could remain subdued. Given that the economic landscape might be changing quickly, the central bank will need to observe developments carefully.

The Committee maintains its view that, considering its objectives of stable prices and sustained economic expansion, and based on current information, the risks are predominantly skewed towards conditions that could lead to economic weakness in the near future.

The Federal Reserve Board approved the requests from the boards of directors of all twelve regional Reserve Banks when implementing the change to the discount rate.

The Committee chose to keep its policy interest rate unchanged.

Recent data indicates a robust employment situation, with consistent job growth over the past few months and a low unemployment rate. Economic activity has been expanding at a moderate pace. While consumer spending has been strong, business investment and exports continue to be weak. Inflation, both overall and excluding volatile components, has been below the 2 percent target over the past year. Market-based indicators of future inflation remain low, and survey-based long-term inflation expectations show little change.

This decision aligns with the Committee's goals of maximizing employment and achieving price stability. The current policy stance is deemed suitable to support ongoing economic expansion, a strong labor market, and inflation around the Committee's symmetric 2 percent objective. The Committee will continue to evaluate new information regarding the economic outlook, including global events and subdued inflation, as it determines the future trajectory of the policy rate.

Future adjustments to the policy rate will depend on an assessment of actual and anticipated economic conditions relative to the Committee's maximum employment and symmetric 2 percent inflation objectives. This evaluation will consider various data, such as labor market indicators, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments.

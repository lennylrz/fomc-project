The Committee decided to keep its primary policy interest rate unchanged.

Recent data indicate an acceleration in economic activity after a significant contraction. Financial market conditions have shown further improvement, and the housing sector has expanded. Consumer spending appears to be stabilizing, though it remains limited by persistent job losses, slow income growth, reduced housing equity, and restricted credit availability. Businesses are still reducing capital expenditures and employment, albeit at a slower rate; they are also making progress in aligning inventory levels with sales.

While economic activity is expected to remain subdued for a period, the Committee foresees that measures taken to stabilize financial markets and institutions, along with fiscal and monetary stimulus and market dynamics, will foster stronger economic expansion and a gradual return to fuller resource utilization, all within an environment of stable prices. Given considerable underutilization of resources, which should continue to restrain cost pressures, and stable long-term inflation expectations, the Committee anticipates that inflation will stay low for some time.

In light of these conditions, the Federal Reserve will continue to utilize various instruments to support economic recovery and maintain price stability. The Committee expects that economic circumstances will necessitate exceptionally low levels of the federal funds rate for an extended duration. To bolster mortgage lending and housing markets, and to enhance overall private credit market conditions, the Federal Reserve will acquire a total of $1.25 trillion in agency mortgage-backed securities and up to $200 billion in agency debt. The Committee plans to gradually reduce the speed of these acquisitions to facilitate an orderly market transition, with completion anticipated by the end of the first quarter of [year+1]. As previously announced, the Federal Reserve's acquisition of $300 billion in Treasury securities will conclude by the end of [month] [this year]. The Committee will continue to assess the timing and total volume of its security acquisitions based on the evolving economic outlook and financial market conditions. The Federal Reserve is observing the scale and composition of its balance sheet and will modify its credit and liquidity programs as needed.

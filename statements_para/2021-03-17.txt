The Committee has chosen to maintain its current policy rate, leaving it unchanged at this meeting.

Recent economic data indicate an uptick in activity and employment following a period of slower recovery, though sectors most impacted by the [event] pandemic remain weak. Inflation continues to fall short of the 2 percent target. Financial conditions generally remain supportive, partly due to policy actions aimed at bolstering the economy and credit availability for households and businesses. The [event] pandemic continues to inflict significant human and economic hardship both domestically and globally.

The economic trajectory is heavily dependent on the virus's progression, including vaccination efforts. The ongoing public health crisis continues to suppress economic activity, employment, and inflation, and presents substantial risks to the future economic outlook.

The Committee aims for maximum employment and a 2 percent inflation rate over the long term. Given persistent inflation below this long-run objective, the Committee intends to allow inflation to moderately surpass 2 percent for a period, ensuring a 2 percent average over time and keeping long-term inflation expectations firmly anchored at 2 percent. An accommodative monetary policy stance will be maintained until these outcomes are achieved. The Committee anticipates that the current policy rate will be appropriate until labor market conditions align with its maximum employment assessments and inflation reaches 2 percent and is on track to moderately exceed 2 percent for some time. Furthermore, the Federal Reserve will continue to expand its holdings of Treasury securities by at least $80 billion monthly and agency mortgage-backed securities by at least $40 billion monthly until significant further progress toward its maximum employment and price stability goals is observed. These asset purchases contribute to smooth market operations and accommodative financial conditions, thereby facilitating credit flow to households and businesses.

In evaluating the appropriate monetary policy stance, the Committee will continue to assess incoming information's implications for the economic outlook. Should risks arise that could hinder the attainment of the Committee's objectives, it stands ready to adjust the monetary policy stance as needed. These assessments will incorporate a broad range of information, including public health data, labor market conditions, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments.

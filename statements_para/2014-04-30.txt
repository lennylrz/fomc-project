The Committee decided to keep its policy interest rate unchanged. It also opted to decrease the monthly pace of its agency mortgage-backed securities purchases by $5 billion and its longer-term Treasury securities purchases by $5 billion, effective [month]. The existing strategy of reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities, will continue.

Recent data indicate that economic activity has accelerated following a significant slowdown during the winter, partly due to unfavorable weather. Labor market indicators presented a mixed picture but, on the whole, showed further progress, though the unemployment rate remains elevated. Household spending appears to be increasing more rapidly. Business fixed investment saw a slight decline, and the housing sector's recovery remained sluggish. Fiscal policy continues to restrain economic growth, though this effect is lessening. Inflation has been below the Committee's long-term target, but long-term inflation expectations have held steady.

The Committee anticipates that, with appropriate policy support, the economy will expand moderately, and labor market conditions will gradually improve, moving toward levels consistent with its dual mandate. The risks to the economic and labor market outlook are considered nearly balanced. The Committee acknowledges that persistently low inflation, below its 2 percent objective, could pose risks to economic performance and is closely monitoring inflation trends for signs of a return to its objective over the medium term.

The Committee believes the economy possesses sufficient underlying strength to sustain ongoing improvements in labor market conditions. Given the cumulative progress toward maximum employment and the improved labor market outlook since the asset purchase program began, a further measured reduction in the pace of asset purchases was deemed appropriate. These substantial and growing holdings of longer-term securities are expected to maintain downward pressure on long-term interest rates, support mortgage markets, and foster more accommodative financial conditions, thereby promoting a stronger economic recovery and helping to ensure inflation aligns with the Committee's dual mandate over time.

The Committee will closely monitor upcoming economic and financial information. It will continue its purchases of Treasury and agency mortgage-backed securities, and utilize other policy tools as needed, until the labor market outlook has substantially improved within a context of price stability. If incoming information generally supports expectations of ongoing labor market improvement and inflation returning to its long-term objective, the Committee will likely reduce the pace of asset purchases in further measured steps at future meetings. However, asset purchases are not predetermined, and decisions on their pace will depend on the Committee's outlook for the labor market and inflation, as well as its assessment of the likely effectiveness and costs of such purchases.

To support continued progress toward maximum employment and price stability, the Committee reaffirmed that a highly accommodative monetary policy stance remains appropriate. In deciding how long to maintain the current target range for the federal funds rate, the Committee will evaluate progress, both realized and anticipated, toward its objectives of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. This assessment will consider various factors, including labor market conditions, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial developments. The Committee continues to expect that, based on these factors, it will likely be appropriate to maintain the current target range for the federal funds rate for a considerable period after the asset purchase program concludes, especially if projected inflation remains below the Committee's 2 percent long-term goal, and provided that long-term inflation expectations remain well anchored.

When the Committee decides to begin removing policy accommodation, it will adopt a balanced approach consistent with its long-term goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. The Committee currently anticipates that, even when employment and inflation are near mandate-consistent levels, economic conditions may, for some time, warrant keeping the target federal funds rate below levels the Committee considers normal in the longer run.

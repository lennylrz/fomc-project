Today, the Committee reduced its primary policy interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point. Concurrently, the Board of Governors sanctioned a corresponding quarter-percentage-point decrease in the discount rate.

The economy continues to exhibit softness. Underlying price increases are expected to decline further from their already moderate levels. While there are initial, tentative indications that the decline in demand might be slowing, these signs are not yet conclusive.

Looking ahead, the Committee assesses that the balance of risks leans towards a period of economic weakness. This evaluation is made in the context of the Committee's objectives for stable prices and sustained economic expansion, and based on current information.

Despite potential short-term constraints on productivity gains due to necessary resource reallocations for security, the long-term outlook for productivity and the broader economy remains positive. This favorable trend should become apparent once the current unusual factors suppressing demand subside.

The Federal Reserve Board's approval of the discount rate adjustment followed requests from the Boards of Directors of the Federal Reserve Banks of Boston, New York, Philadelphia, Chicago, and San Francisco.

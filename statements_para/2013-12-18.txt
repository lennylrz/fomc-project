The Committee decided to keep its policy interest rate unchanged. It also opted to moderately decrease the pace of its asset acquisition program. Starting in [month], the monthly purchase of agency mortgage-backed securities will be reduced by $5 billion, and the monthly purchase of longer-term Treasury securities will also be reduced by $5 billion. The existing practice of reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities, will continue. These substantial and growing holdings are intended to keep long-term interest rates low, support the housing finance market, and generally ease financial conditions, thereby fostering a stronger economic recovery and helping to achieve the 2 percent inflation objective over time.

Recent data, since the [month] meeting, indicate that economic activity is expanding at a moderate rate. The job market has shown further improvement, with a decline in the unemployment rate, though it remains elevated. Household spending and business investment have increased. However, the housing sector's recovery has slowed somewhat in recent months. Fiscal policy continues to restrain economic growth, though this effect may be lessening. Inflation has been below the Committee's long-term objective, but long-term inflation expectations have remained stable.

The Committee believes that, with appropriate policy support, economic growth will accelerate from its current pace, and the unemployment rate will gradually fall to levels consistent with its dual mandate of maximum employment and price stability. The risks to the economic and labor market outlook are now considered more balanced. The Committee is closely monitoring inflation developments, as persistently low inflation could pose risks to economic performance, to ensure it moves back towards the 2 percent objective over the medium term.

The Committee will continue to monitor incoming economic and financial information. It will maintain its purchases of Treasury and agency mortgage-backed securities, and use other policy tools as needed, until the labor market outlook has substantially improved within a context of price stability. If incoming data generally support the expectation of ongoing labor market improvement and inflation returning to its long-run objective, the Committee will likely reduce the pace of asset purchases in further gradual steps at future meetings. However, asset purchases are not on a predetermined path; decisions on their pace will depend on the Committee's outlook for the labor market and inflation, as well as its assessment of the effectiveness and costs of such purchases.

To support continued progress toward maximum employment and price stability, the Committee reaffirmed that a highly accommodative monetary policy stance will be appropriate for a considerable period after the asset purchase program concludes and the economic recovery strengthens. The Committee also reiterated its expectation that the current exceptionally low target range for the federal funds rate will be appropriate at least as long as the unemployment rate remains above 6-1/2 percent, projected inflation one to two years ahead is no more than a half percentage point above the 2 percent long-run goal, and long-term inflation expectations remain well anchored. In deciding how long to maintain this highly accommodative stance, the Committee will also consider other information, including additional labor market measures, indicators of inflation pressures and expectations, and financial developments. Based on these factors, the Committee now anticipates that maintaining the current target range for the federal funds rate will likely be appropriate well past the time the unemployment rate falls below 6-1/2 percent, particularly if projected inflation continues to run below the 2 percent long-run goal. When the Committee decides to begin removing policy accommodation, it will take a balanced approach consistent with its long-run goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation.

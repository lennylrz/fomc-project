The Committee decided to keep its policy interest rate unchanged.

Recent data indicate a continued economic downturn, though at a somewhat slower pace. Consumer spending shows signs of stabilization but is still hampered by job losses, reduced housing wealth, and limited credit access. Businesses are cutting inventories, investment, and staff due to poor sales prospects and credit difficulties.

While the economic outlook has slightly improved since [month], partly due to some easing in financial markets, economic activity is expected to remain weak for a period. However, the Committee still believes that measures to stabilize financial markets and institutions, along with fiscal and monetary stimulus and market forces, will eventually lead to sustainable economic growth with stable prices.

Given the significant economic slack domestically and internationally, the Committee anticipates that inflation will stay low. There is also a risk that inflation could remain below optimal levels for fostering long-term economic growth and price stability.

Under these conditions, the Federal Reserve will utilize all available instruments to support economic recovery and maintain price stability. The Committee expects that economic circumstances will necessitate exceptionally low policy rates for an extended duration. As previously announced, to support mortgage lending, housing markets, and overall private credit conditions, the Federal Reserve will acquire up to $1.25 trillion in agency mortgage-backed securities and up to $200 billion in agency debt by year-end. Additionally, up to $300 billion in Treasury securities will be purchased by autumn. The Committee will continue to assess the timing and total volume of these security purchases based on the evolving economic outlook and financial market conditions. The Federal Reserve is also facilitating credit extension to households and businesses and supporting financial market function through various liquidity programs. The Committee will continue to closely monitor the size and composition of the Federal Reserve's balance sheet in response to financial and economic developments.

The Committee decided to increase its primary policy interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point.

Recent data indicates that the economy has been growing at a moderate speed. Employment has seen strong increases recently, and the jobless rate has stayed low. Price increases are still high. The banking system in the United States is robust and stable. Stricter lending conditions for consumers and businesses will probably slow economic activity, job creation, and inflation. The full impact of these changes is not yet clear. The Committee remains very focused on the dangers posed by inflation.

The Committee aims for maximum employment and a 2 percent inflation rate over the long term. To support these objectives, the Committee will keep evaluating new information and what it means for monetary policy. When deciding how much more policy tightening might be needed to bring inflation back to 2 percent eventually, the Committee will consider the total amount of policy tightening already done, the time it takes for monetary policy to affect the economy and inflation, and current economic and financial conditions. Furthermore, the Committee will proceed with reducing its holdings of government bonds and agency debt and mortgage-backed securities, as previously announced. The Committee is firmly dedicated to achieving its 2 percent inflation target.

In evaluating the correct approach to monetary policy, the Committee will keep an eye on what new information implies for the economic outlook. The Committee would be ready to adjust its monetary policy stance if new risks arise that could hinder the achievement of its goals. The Committee's evaluations will consider a broad range of information, including labor market indicators, inflationary pressures and expectations, and financial and global developments.

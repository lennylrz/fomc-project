The Committee decided to keep its policy interest rate unchanged.

Recent data indicate a deceleration in the rate of economic and employment recovery. Consumer spending is slowly increasing, but remains limited by elevated joblessness, modest income growth, reduced housing wealth, and restricted credit access. Business investment in equipment and software is growing, though at a slower pace than earlier in the year, and nonresidential construction investment remains weak. Businesses are hesitant to expand their workforces. New home construction is at a low level. Bank lending continues to shrink, but at a slower rate recently. The Committee foresees a gradual improvement in resource utilization while maintaining stable prices, though the near-term economic recovery will likely be modest.

Underlying inflation measures are currently somewhat below the levels the Committee deems most appropriate for achieving its dual mandate of maximum employment and price stability over the long run. Given significant unused capacity restraining cost pressures and stable long-term inflation expectations, inflation is expected to remain low for some time before reaching target levels.

The Committee will maintain its current approach of reinvesting principal payments from its securities. It continues to expect that prevailing economic conditions, including low resource utilization, subdued inflation, and stable inflation expectations, will necessitate exceptionally low policy rates for an extended duration.

The Committee will continue to observe economic and financial developments and stands ready to offer further support if needed to bolster the economic recovery and guide inflation back to its mandated levels over time.

One member, [member], dissented from this policy. [member] believed the economy is recovering at a moderate pace and therefore found the expectation of exceptionally low policy rates for an extended period to be unwarranted, potentially leading to future imbalances that could hinder stable long-term growth. Furthermore, [member] did not believe that continuing to reinvest principal payments from securities was necessary given current economic and financial conditions to achieve the Committee's objectives.

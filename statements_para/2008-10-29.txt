The Committee has opted to decrease its primary interest rate by half a percentage point. Concurrently, the Board of Governors unanimously endorsed a similar half-percentage-point reduction in the discount rate, responding to proposals from the Federal Reserve Banks of Boston, New York, Cleveland, and San Francisco.

Economic activity appears to have decelerated significantly, largely due to a reduction in household spending. Business investment in equipment and factory output have softened recently, and slower economic expansion in numerous international economies is dampening the outlook for American exports. Furthermore, the worsening financial market instability is expected to further constrain spending, partly by making it harder for households and businesses to access credit.

Given the fall in energy and other commodity prices, alongside a weaker economic outlook, the Committee anticipates that inflation will ease in the upcoming quarters to levels consistent with stable prices.

Recent policy interventions, including today's rate adjustment, synchronized interest rate cuts by other central banks, exceptional liquidity provisions, and official measures to bolster financial systems, should gradually help improve credit conditions and foster a return to moderate economic expansion. However, risks to growth remain tilted to the downside. The Committee will closely observe economic and financial trends and will intervene as necessary to support sustained economic growth and price stability.

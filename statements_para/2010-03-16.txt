The Committee decided to keep its policy rate unchanged.

Recent data indicates ongoing economic strengthening, with the job market showing signs of stabilization. Consumer spending is growing moderately, though held back by elevated joblessness, limited income gains, reduced housing wealth, and restricted credit access. Business investment in equipment and software has increased considerably. However, commercial construction is declining, housing starts remain flat at a low level, and companies are hesitant to hire. While bank lending continues to shrink, financial conditions generally support economic expansion. The Committee expects a gradual return to fuller economic utilization, with stable prices, despite a likely moderate pace of recovery for some time.

With significant unused capacity limiting cost pressures and long-term inflation expectations steady, inflation is expected to remain low for a period.

The Committee will maintain its current policy rate and anticipates that prevailing economic conditions, including underutilized resources, low inflation trends, and stable inflation expectations, will necessitate exceptionally low policy rates for an extended duration. To aid mortgage markets and overall private credit, the Federal Reserve has been acquiring agency mortgage-backed securities and agency debt; these acquisitions are almost finished, with remaining transactions concluding by the end of this month. The Committee will continue to observe economic and financial developments, using its tools as needed to foster recovery and price stability.

Given improved financial market functioning, the Federal Reserve has been phasing out its special liquidity programs.

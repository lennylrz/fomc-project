The Federal Open Market Committee has opted to decrease its primary interest rate by half a percentage point. This adjustment reflects a collective decision among several central banks to ease monetary conditions globally.

This action was taken due to indications of slowing economic expansion and a reduction in price pressures. Recent economic figures suggest a notable deceleration in activity over the past few months. Furthermore, the worsening financial market instability is expected to further curb spending, partly by making it harder for households and businesses to access credit. While inflation has been elevated, the Committee anticipates that falling energy and commodity prices, alongside a less favorable economic outlook, have lessened the upward risks to inflation.

The Committee will closely observe economic and financial trends and will intervene as necessary to foster sustained economic growth and stable prices.

Separately, the Board of Governors unanimously endorsed a half-percentage-point reduction in the discount rate.

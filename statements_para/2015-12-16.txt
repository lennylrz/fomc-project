The Committee decided to increase its policy interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point.

Since the [month] meeting, economic activity has expanded at a moderate rate. Household spending and business investment have shown solid growth, and the housing sector has improved. However, net exports have been weak. The labor market has continued to improve, with ongoing job gains and declining unemployment indicating a significant reduction in underutilization of labor resources since early this year. Inflation remains below the 2 percent objective, partly due to lower energy and non-energy import prices. Market-based measures of inflation compensation are low, and some survey-based long-term inflation expectations have slightly decreased.

The Committee anticipates that, with gradual adjustments to monetary policy, economic activity will continue its moderate expansion, and labor market indicators will strengthen further. Considering both domestic and international factors, the Committee views the risks to the outlook for economic activity and the labor market as balanced. Inflation is projected to reach 2 percent over the medium term as the temporary effects of lower energy and import prices fade and the labor market improves. The Committee is closely monitoring inflation developments.

The Committee believes that labor market conditions have improved considerably this year and is reasonably confident that inflation will reach its 2 percent objective over the medium term. Given the economic outlook and the time it takes for policy actions to affect future economic outcomes, the policy stance remains accommodative after this increase, supporting further labor market improvement and a return to 2 percent inflation.

Future adjustments to the policy rate will depend on an assessment of economic conditions relative to maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. This assessment will consider labor market conditions, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments. Given the current inflation shortfall, the Committee will carefully monitor progress toward its inflation goal. The Committee expects that economic conditions will warrant only gradual increases in the policy rate, which is likely to remain below long-run levels for some time. However, the actual path of the policy rate will depend on incoming economic data.

The Committee will continue its existing policy of reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities and rolling over maturing Treasury securities. This is expected to continue until the normalization of the policy rate is well underway, maintaining accommodative financial conditions by keeping the Committee's holdings of longer-term securities at sizable levels.

The Committee chose to decrease its policy interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point.

This decision follows an assessment of recent economic data. The job market continues to exhibit strength, and overall economic activity has seen a moderate increase. Employment growth has been consistent over recent periods, and the unemployment rate remains low. While consumer spending has shown robust growth, business investment in fixed assets and exports have softened. Looking at the past year, both headline inflation and core inflation (excluding food and energy) are below the 2 percent target. Market indicators of expected inflation are still low, and surveys of long-term inflation expectations have shown little change.

This adjustment is intended to support the Committee's expectation of continued economic expansion, a robust labor market, and inflation stabilizing around its symmetric 2 percent goal, despite ongoing uncertainties.

Regarding future policy adjustments, the Committee will continue to observe how new information affects the economic outlook. It will take necessary steps to maintain the expansion, a strong labor market, and inflation near its symmetric 2 percent objective. When considering future changes to the policy rate, the Committee will evaluate current and projected economic conditions against its goals of maximum employment and symmetric 2 percent inflation. This evaluation will incorporate various data points, including labor market metrics, inflation indicators and expectations, and financial and global developments.

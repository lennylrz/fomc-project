The Committee chose to maintain its primary policy rate at its current level.

Economic expansion has slowed from its earlier robust pace this year. This moderation is partly due to a gradual cooling in the housing sector and the delayed impact of prior interest rate hikes and energy cost increases.

Recent data indicate that underlying inflation has been elevated. The current high utilization of economic resources, coupled with elevated energy and commodity prices, could sustain inflationary pressures. However, inflation is expected to ease over time, given stable inflation expectations and the combined effects of monetary policy actions and other factors that are curbing overall demand.

Despite these expectations, the Committee believes that some inflation risks persist. The necessity, magnitude, and timing of any further policy tightening to address these risks will be determined by how the outlook for both inflation and economic growth evolves, based on new information.

One member, [member], dissented from this decision, preferring a quarter-percentage-point increase in the policy rate.

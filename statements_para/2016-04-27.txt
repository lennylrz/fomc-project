The Committee chose to keep its policy interest rate unchanged at this meeting. This decision reflects an accommodative monetary policy stance, which supports ongoing improvements in the job market and a return to the 2 percent inflation target.

Recent data since the [month] meeting indicate further strengthening in the labor market, despite a perceived slowdown in overall economic activity. Household spending growth has moderated, even as real incomes have increased solidly and consumer confidence remains high. The housing sector has shown continued improvement this year, though business fixed investment and net exports have been weak. Various recent indicators, including robust job creation, suggest further gains in the labor market. Inflation continues to be below the Committee's 2 percent long-term objective, partly due to earlier declines in energy prices and cheaper non-energy imports. Market-based inflation compensation measures are still low, while survey-based long-term inflation expectations have shown little net change recently.

The Committee anticipates that, with gradual adjustments to monetary policy, economic activity will expand moderately, and labor market indicators will continue to improve. Inflation is expected to stay low in the near term, partly due to past energy price drops, but should rise to 2 percent over the medium term as temporary effects from energy and import price declines fade and the labor market strengthens further. The Committee continues to closely observe inflation indicators and global economic and financial developments.

Future adjustments to the policy rate's target range will depend on an assessment of actual and projected economic conditions against the goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. This evaluation will consider a broad range of information, including labor market metrics, inflation pressure and expectation indicators, and financial and international developments. Given the current inflation shortfall from 2 percent, the Committee will carefully monitor progress toward its inflation goal. The Committee expects economic conditions to warrant only gradual increases in the policy rate, which is likely to remain below its long-run expected level for some time. However, the actual path of the policy rate will be guided by the economic outlook and incoming data.

The Committee will continue its current policy of reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities. This is expected to continue until the normalization of the policy rate is well underway. This approach, by maintaining substantial holdings of longer-term securities, should help keep financial conditions accommodative.

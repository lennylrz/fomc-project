The Committee chose to maintain its primary policy interest rate at its current level. It also initiated a reduction in the monthly acquisition of Treasury bonds by $10 billion and mortgage-backed securities by $5 billion. This adjustment means that starting later this month, the central bank will acquire at least $70 billion in Treasury securities and $35 billion in mortgage-backed securities each month. From [month] onward, these figures will decrease to at least $60 billion for Treasury securities and $30 billion for mortgage-backed securities monthly. The Committee anticipates similar reductions in the pace of these purchases in subsequent months, but stands ready to modify this approach if economic conditions warrant.

Economic activity and employment indicators have shown continued improvement, supported by vaccination efforts and robust policy measures. Sectors previously hit hardest by the pandemic have recovered somewhat, though the summer surge in [event] cases has slowed their progress. Inflation is currently high, primarily due to factors expected to be temporary. Supply and demand imbalances, stemming from the pandemic and economic reopening, have led to significant price increases in certain areas. Overall financial conditions remain supportive, partly due to policies aimed at bolstering the economy and credit availability.

The economic trajectory remains dependent on the pandemic's evolution. Increased vaccination rates and easing supply constraints are projected to foster ongoing economic and employment growth, alongside a moderation in inflation. However, the economic outlook still faces various uncertainties.

The Committee aims for maximum employment and a long-term inflation rate of 2 percent. Given that inflation has consistently been below this target, the Committee intends to allow inflation to moderately exceed 2 percent for a period, ensuring a 2 percent average over time and keeping long-term inflation expectations anchored at 2 percent. An accommodative monetary policy stance will be maintained until these objectives are met. The Committee expects to keep its policy rate unchanged until labor market conditions align with its assessment of maximum employment, and inflation reaches 2 percent and is on track to moderately surpass it for some time. The decision to reduce asset purchases reflects the substantial progress the economy has made toward the Committee's goals since last [month]. The ongoing asset purchases and holdings will continue to promote smooth market functioning and accommodative financial conditions, thereby facilitating credit flow to households and businesses.

The Committee will continue to evaluate incoming data to assess its implications for the economic outlook. It is prepared to adjust its policy stance if risks emerge that could hinder the achievement of its objectives. This assessment will incorporate a broad range of information, including public health data, labor market conditions, inflation pressures and expectations, and global financial developments.

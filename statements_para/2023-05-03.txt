The Committee chose to increase its primary interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point at this meeting.

Economic activity showed modest growth during the initial three months of the year. The labor market has seen strong employment gains recently, and joblessness has stayed low. Price increases are still high. The American banking system is robust and stable. It is probable that stricter lending conditions for consumers and businesses will negatively impact economic activity, employment, and inflation. The full impact of these changes is not yet clear. The Committee remains very focused on the dangers posed by inflation.

The Committee aims for maximum employment and a 2 percent inflation rate over the long term. To support these objectives, the Committee will continue to decrease its holdings of government bonds and mortgage-backed securities, following its established strategy. The Committee is firmly dedicated to bringing inflation back to its 2 percent target.

Regarding future policy, the Committee will carefully observe new data and evaluate its implications for monetary policy. When deciding if further policy tightening is needed to bring inflation back to 2 percent eventually, the Committee will consider the total amount of monetary policy tightening already implemented, the delayed effects of monetary policy on economic activity and inflation, and overall economic and financial trends. The Committee will also be ready to modify its policy stance if new risks arise that could hinder the achievement of its goals. These evaluations will incorporate a broad range of information, including labor market indicators, inflation pressures and expectations, and global financial developments.

The Committee decided to keep its policy interest rate unchanged. It also opted to reduce the monthly pace of its agency mortgage-backed securities purchases by $5 billion and its longer-term Treasury securities purchases by $5 billion, starting in [month]. The existing policy of reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities, will continue.

Recent data indicates that economic activity is expanding at a moderate rate. The labor market has shown some improvement overall, though the unemployment rate is largely stable, and various indicators suggest significant underutilization of labor resources persists. Consumer spending appears to be increasing moderately, and business fixed investment is advancing. However, the housing sector's recovery remains slow. Fiscal policy continues to restrain economic growth, though this effect is lessening. Inflation has been below the Committee's long-term objective, while long-term inflation expectations have remained stable.

The Committee expects that, with appropriate policy support, economic activity will grow moderately, and labor market indicators and inflation will move toward levels consistent with its dual mandate of maximum employment and price stability. The risks to the economic and labor market outlook are considered nearly balanced. The probability of inflation remaining persistently below 2 percent has decreased somewhat since early this year.

The Committee believes there is sufficient underlying strength in the economy to support ongoing improvement in labor market conditions. Given the progress toward maximum employment and the improved labor market outlook since the asset purchase program began, a further measured reduction in asset purchases was deemed appropriate. The substantial and growing holdings of long-term securities are expected to maintain downward pressure on long-term interest rates, support mortgage markets, and foster more accommodative financial conditions, thereby promoting a stronger economic recovery and helping to ensure inflation reaches the 2 percent objective over time.

The Committee will closely monitor incoming economic and financial information. It will continue its asset purchases and use other policy tools as needed until the labor market outlook has substantially improved within a context of price stability. If incoming data generally supports the Committee's expectation of ongoing labor market improvement and inflation moving toward its long-run objective, the asset purchase program will conclude at the next meeting. However, asset purchases are not on a predetermined path; decisions about their pace will depend on the Committee's outlook for the labor market and inflation, as well as its assessment of the efficacy and costs of such purchases.

A highly accommodative monetary policy stance remains appropriate to support progress toward maximum employment and price stability. The Committee will assess progress toward its objectives of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation, both realized and expected, when determining how long to maintain the current policy rate. This assessment will consider labor market conditions, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial developments. The Committee anticipates that the current policy rate will likely be maintained for a considerable time after the asset purchase program ends, especially if projected inflation remains below the 2 percent long-run goal and long-term inflation expectations remain well anchored.

When policy accommodation is eventually removed, a balanced approach consistent with the long-run goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation will be adopted. Even after employment and inflation are near mandate-consistent levels, economic conditions may warrant keeping the policy rate below normal long-run levels for some time.

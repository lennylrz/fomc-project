The Committee chose to keep its policy interest rate unchanged. It will also continue to decrease its holdings of government and agency-backed securities according to its established strategy.

Economic activity has recently shown a deceleration from its robust performance in the third quarter. The labor market continues to add jobs at a healthy pace, though not as rapidly as earlier in the year, and joblessness remains low. Price increases have moderated over the past year but are still high. The U.S. banking sector is robust and stable.

Looking ahead, tighter financial conditions and credit availability for consumers and businesses are expected to restrain economic activity, employment, and inflation. The full impact of these factors is not yet clear. The Committee remains very focused on the dangers posed by inflation.

The Committee aims for maximum employment and a 2 percent inflation rate over the long term. To achieve these objectives, the Committee will evaluate new data and its implications for monetary policy. When considering any further policy adjustments needed to bring inflation back to 2 percent, the Committee will consider the total amount of policy tightening already implemented, the delayed effects of monetary policy on the economy and inflation, and evolving economic and financial conditions.

The Committee will keep an eye on how new information affects the economic outlook when deciding on the appropriate policy stance. Should new risks arise that could hinder the Committee's objectives, it stands ready to adjust monetary policy as needed. These evaluations will incorporate a broad spectrum of data, including labor market indicators, inflationary pressures and expectations, and global financial developments.

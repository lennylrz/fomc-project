The Committee decided to increase its policy interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point.

Recent data show continued strengthening in the job market and robust economic expansion. Employment gains have been substantial over recent months, leading to a lower unemployment rate. Consumer spending has accelerated, and business investment in fixed assets remains strong. Over the past year, both headline and core inflation measures are approaching the 2 percent target. Long-term inflation expectations have remained largely stable.

The Committee anticipates that additional, measured increases in the policy rate will support ongoing economic growth, a healthy job market, and inflation stabilizing around its symmetric 2 percent goal in the medium term. The risks to the economic outlook are generally balanced.

This policy adjustment, which keeps monetary conditions supportive, aims to sustain a strong labor market and guide inflation back to 2 percent.

Future adjustments to the policy rate will depend on an evaluation of current and projected economic conditions against the Committee's goals of maximum employment and symmetric 2 percent inflation. This evaluation will consider various data, including labor market indicators, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and global developments.

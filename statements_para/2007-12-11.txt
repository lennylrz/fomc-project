The Committee has opted to decrease its primary policy interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point. Concurrently, the Board of Governors unanimously endorsed a 25-basis-point reduction in the discount rate.

Recent data indicate a deceleration in economic expansion, driven by a more pronounced downturn in the housing sector and a slight weakening in both business and household expenditures. Furthermore, financial market tensions have escalated over the past few weeks. This adjustment, alongside previous policy interventions, is expected to support moderate economic expansion over time. While underlying inflation measures have shown some improvement this year, elevated costs for energy and raw materials, among other elements, could exert upward pressure on overall price levels.

The Committee believes that some inflation-related risks persist and will closely track price developments. Recent events, including the worsening of financial market conditions, have heightened the unpredictability surrounding future economic growth and inflation. The Committee will continue to evaluate how financial and other factors influence economic prospects and will take necessary steps to maintain stable prices and sustained economic expansion.

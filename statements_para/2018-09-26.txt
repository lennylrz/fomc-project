The Committee decided to increase its policy interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point.

Recent data since the [month] gathering shows continued improvement in the employment situation and robust economic expansion. Employment figures have shown consistent strength over recent periods, and joblessness remains at a low level. Consumer expenditures and corporate capital outlays have both exhibited vigorous growth. Over the past year, both headline inflation and core inflation, which excludes volatile food and energy prices, are close to the 2 percent target. Measures of long-term inflation expectations have generally held steady.

The Committee anticipates that additional, measured increases in the policy rate will support ongoing economic growth, a healthy job market, and inflation stabilizing around its 2 percent target over the medium term. The potential threats to the economic outlook appear to be evenly distributed.

Future adjustments to the policy rate will depend on the Committee's evaluation of current and projected economic conditions against its goals of maximum employment and stable prices. This evaluation will incorporate various data points, such as labor market indicators, inflation pressures and expectations, and global financial trends.

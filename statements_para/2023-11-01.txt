The Committee chose to keep its primary policy interest rate unchanged at this meeting. This decision supports the Committee's objectives of fostering maximum employment and achieving a 2 percent inflation rate over the long term.

Regarding the economic situation, recent data indicates robust expansion in economic activity during the third quarter. While job creation has slowed compared to earlier in the year, it continues to be strong, and the unemployment rate has stayed low. Inflationary pressures persist at an elevated level. The U.S. banking sector is robust and stable. It is anticipated that more restrictive financial and credit conditions for both consumers and businesses will likely dampen economic activity, employment, and inflation. The full impact of these conditions is not yet clear. The Committee remains very focused on the dangers posed by inflation.

The Committee will persist in evaluating new information and its implications for monetary policy. When considering whether further policy tightening is needed to bring inflation back to 2 percent over time, the Committee will consider the total amount of monetary policy tightening already implemented, the delayed effects of monetary policy on economic activity and inflation, and evolving economic and financial conditions. Furthermore, the Committee will continue to decrease its holdings of government bonds and agency mortgage-backed securities as previously outlined. The Committee is firmly dedicated to achieving its 2 percent inflation target.

In determining the appropriate monetary policy stance, the Committee will continue to observe how incoming data affects the economic outlook. Should risks arise that could hinder the Committee's objectives, it stands ready to adjust the monetary policy stance as necessary. These assessments will incorporate a broad spectrum of information, including labor market indicators, inflation pressures and expectations, and developments in financial markets and internationally.

The Committee decided to maintain its policy interest rate at its current level. It also chose to decrease the monthly pace of its agency mortgage-backed securities purchases by $5 billion and its longer-term Treasury securities purchases by $5 billion. The existing practice of reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities, will continue.

Recent data indicates that economic expansion slowed during the winter months, partly due to unfavorable weather. Labor market indicators presented a mixed picture but, on the whole, showed further improvement, though the unemployment rate remains elevated. Household spending and business investment continued to grow, while the housing sector's recovery was sluggish. Fiscal policy is constraining economic growth, though this effect is lessening. Inflation has been below the Committee's long-term target, but long-term inflation expectations have remained stable.

The Committee believes that the economy possesses sufficient underlying strength to support ongoing improvements in the labor market. The Committee expects moderate economic growth and gradual improvement in labor market conditions, moving towards its dual mandate objectives, with appropriate policy support. Risks to the economic and labor market outlook are considered nearly balanced. The Committee acknowledges that persistent inflation below its 2 percent objective could negatively impact economic performance and is closely monitoring inflation trends for signs of a return to target over the medium term.

The Committee will closely monitor incoming economic and financial information. It will continue its asset purchases and use other policy tools as needed until the labor market outlook has substantially improved within a stable price environment. If incoming data generally supports expectations of ongoing labor market improvement and inflation returning to its long-run objective, the Committee will likely reduce the pace of asset purchases in further measured steps at future meetings. However, asset purchases are not predetermined, and decisions about their pace will depend on the Committee's outlook for the labor market and inflation, as well as its assessment of the effectiveness and costs of such purchases.

To support progress toward maximum employment and price stability, the Committee reaffirmed that a highly accommodative monetary policy stance remains appropriate. The Committee will assess progress toward its maximum employment and 2 percent inflation objectives, both realized and expected, when determining how long to maintain the current policy interest rate range. This assessment will consider various information, including labor market conditions, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial developments. The Committee anticipates that, based on these factors, it will likely be appropriate to maintain the current policy interest rate range for a considerable time after the asset purchase program concludes, especially if projected inflation remains below the 2 percent long-run goal and long-term inflation expectations remain stable. Even after employment and inflation are near mandate-consistent levels, economic conditions may, for some time, warrant keeping the policy interest rate below levels the Committee views as normal in the longer run.

With the unemployment rate approaching 6-1/2 percent, the Committee has updated its forward guidance. This change does not alter the Committee's policy intentions as outlined in previous statements.

One member, [member], dissented from this action. While supporting the asset purchase adjustment, this member believed that the guidance regarding the policy interest rate weakens the Committee's commitment to returning inflation to the 2 percent target from below and creates policy uncertainty that hinders economic activity.

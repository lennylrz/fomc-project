The Committee has opted to keep its policy interest rate unchanged.

Recent data indicates continued strengthening in the job market and robust economic expansion. Employment figures have shown consistent gains recently, and the unemployment rate remains low. Consumer spending continues to rise significantly, though business investment growth has slowed from its earlier rapid pace last year. Both overall inflation and core inflation, excluding food and energy, are still close to the 2 percent target over the past year. While market-based indicators of future inflation have decreased recently, survey-based measures of long-term inflation expectations are largely stable.

The Committee anticipates that sustained economic growth, a strong labor market, and inflation near its symmetric 2 percent goal are the most probable future developments. Given global economic and financial conditions, alongside subdued inflationary pressures, the Committee will proceed cautiously in deciding on any future adjustments to the policy rate to support these outcomes.

Future decisions regarding the timing and magnitude of policy rate changes will depend on an evaluation of current and projected economic conditions against the Committee's objectives for maximum employment and symmetric 2 percent inflation. This evaluation will incorporate various data, including labor market metrics, inflation indicators and expectations, and financial and international developments.

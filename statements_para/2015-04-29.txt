The Committee decided to maintain its current stance on the federal funds rate, keeping it unchanged at this meeting. It also affirmed its ongoing strategy of reinvesting principal from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities. This approach aims to sustain supportive financial conditions by preserving substantial holdings of longer-term assets.

Recent data indicates a slowdown in economic expansion during the winter months, partly due to temporary influences. Job creation eased, and the unemployment rate held steady. Various labor market metrics suggest little change in the underutilization of labor resources. Household spending growth decreased, despite a strong rise in real incomes, partly from earlier energy price drops, and continued high consumer confidence. Business fixed investment softened, the housing sector's recovery remained sluggish, and exports declined. Inflation stayed below the Committee's long-term target, partly reflecting previous declines in energy prices and falling non-energy import costs. Market-based inflation compensation measures remain low, while survey-based long-term inflation expectations have been stable.

Despite the first-quarter deceleration in output and employment, the Committee still expects moderate economic growth with appropriate policy support. Labor market indicators are projected to continue moving towards levels consistent with its dual mandate. The Committee views the risks to the economic and labor market outlook as nearly balanced. Inflation is expected to stay near its current low level in the near term but should gradually approach 2 percent over the medium term as the labor market strengthens and the temporary effects of energy and import price declines fade. The Committee will continue to closely monitor inflation trends.

The Committee anticipates that an increase in the federal funds rate target range will be appropriate once further labor market improvement is observed and there is reasonable confidence that inflation will return to its 2 percent objective over the medium term. This assessment will consider a broad array of information, including labor market conditions, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments. When the Committee eventually begins to withdraw policy accommodation, it will adopt a balanced approach aligned with its long-term goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. Even when employment and inflation are near mandate-consistent levels, economic conditions may, for some time, necessitate keeping the federal funds rate below levels considered normal in the long run.

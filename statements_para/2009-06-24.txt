The Committee decided to keep its policy interest rate unchanged.

Recent data indicate a deceleration in the rate of economic decline. Financial market conditions have generally improved over the past few months. Consumer spending shows further signs of stabilization but is still limited by ongoing job reductions, reduced housing equity, and restricted credit availability. Businesses are reducing capital expenditures and employment, yet they seem to be making headway in aligning inventory levels with sales. While economic activity is expected to stay weak for a period, the Committee still believes that measures to stabilize financial markets and institutions, along with fiscal and monetary stimulus and market dynamics, will foster a gradual return to sustained economic expansion with stable prices.

Energy and other commodity prices have recently increased. Nevertheless, significant underutilization of resources is expected to mitigate cost pressures, and the Committee anticipates that inflation will remain low for some time.

Given these conditions, the Federal Reserve will utilize all available instruments to support economic recovery and maintain price stability. The Committee expects that economic circumstances will necessitate exceptionally low policy rates for an extended duration. As previously announced, to bolster mortgage lending and housing markets and enhance overall private credit market conditions, the Federal Reserve will acquire up to $1.25 trillion in agency mortgage-backed securities and up to $200 billion in agency debt by year-end. Additionally, the Federal Reserve will purchase up to $300 billion in Treasury securities by autumn. The Committee will continue to assess the timing and total volume of its security purchases based on the evolving economic outlook and financial market conditions. The Federal Reserve is monitoring its balance sheet's size and composition and will modify its credit and liquidity programs as needed.

The Committee decided to maintain its primary policy interest rate at its current setting. It also chose to reduce the monthly pace of its agency mortgage-backed securities acquisitions by $5 billion and its longer-term Treasury securities acquisitions by $5 billion, effective in [month]. The existing practice of reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities, will continue.

Recent data indicate a resurgence in economic activity. The job market has shown further positive developments, though the unemployment rate, while lower, remains elevated. Household spending is increasing moderately, and business investment has resumed its upward trend. The housing sector's recovery remains sluggish. Fiscal policy continues to constrain economic expansion, but this effect is lessening. Inflation has been below the Committee's long-term target, but long-term inflation expectations are stable.

The Committee anticipates that, with appropriate monetary support, the economy will grow at a moderate pace, and labor market conditions will gradually improve, aligning with its dual mandate. The risks to the economic and labor market outlook are considered nearly balanced. Persistent inflation below 2 percent could negatively impact economic performance, and the Committee is closely monitoring inflation trends to ensure it returns to target over the medium term.

The Committee believes the economy possesses sufficient underlying strength to support ongoing improvements in the labor market. Given the cumulative progress toward maximum employment and the improved labor market outlook since the asset purchase program began, a further measured reduction in the pace of asset purchases is warranted. These substantial and still-growing holdings of long-term securities are expected to keep long-term interest rates low, support mortgage markets, and foster more accommodative financial conditions, thereby promoting a stronger recovery and helping inflation reach the 2 percent objective over time.

The Committee will continue to monitor economic and financial developments and will maintain its asset purchases and other policy tools until the labor market outlook has substantially improved within a context of price stability. If incoming information generally supports expectations of ongoing labor market improvement and inflation returning to its long-run objective, the Committee will likely reduce the pace of asset purchases in further measured steps. However, the pace of asset purchases is not predetermined and will depend on the Committee's outlook for the labor market and inflation, as well as the efficacy and costs of such purchases.

A highly accommodative monetary policy stance remains appropriate to support progress toward maximum employment and price stability. The Committee will assess progress toward its objectives of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation when determining how long to maintain the current federal funds rate target range. This assessment will consider various factors, including labor market indicators, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial developments. The Committee expects that the current federal funds rate target range will likely be maintained for a considerable time after the asset purchase program concludes, especially if projected inflation remains below 2 percent and long-term inflation expectations remain anchored.

When the Committee begins to remove policy accommodation, it will adopt a balanced approach consistent with its long-run goals. Even after employment and inflation are near mandate-consistent levels, economic conditions may warrant keeping the federal funds rate below normal long-run levels for some time.

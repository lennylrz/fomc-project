The Committee decided to keep its policy interest rate unchanged. It also chose to reduce the monthly pace of its Treasury bond purchases by $5 billion and its mortgage-backed securities purchases by $5 billion, effective in [month]. The existing strategy of reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities, will continue.

Recent data indicates that economic activity has accelerated in recent quarters. Labor market indicators presented a mixed picture but, on balance, showed further improvement. The unemployment rate has decreased but remains elevated. Household spending and business fixed investment have grown more quickly recently, though the housing sector's recovery has slowed somewhat. Fiscal policy continues to restrain economic growth, but this effect is lessening. Inflation has been below the Committee's long-term objective, yet long-term inflation expectations have remained stable.

The Committee believes that, with appropriate policy support, economic activity will expand moderately, and the unemployment rate will gradually fall towards levels consistent with its dual mandate. The risks to the economic and labor market outlook are now seen as more balanced. The Committee recognizes that persistently low inflation could pose risks to economic performance and is closely monitoring inflation developments to ensure it moves back towards its 2 percent objective over the medium term.

The Committee expects that if incoming information broadly supports ongoing improvements in labor market conditions and inflation returning to its long-term objective, it will likely reduce the pace of asset purchases in further measured steps at future meetings. However, asset purchases are not on a predetermined path, and decisions about their pace will depend on the Committee's outlook for the labor market and inflation, as well as its assessment of the efficacy and costs of such purchases.

The Committee reaffirmed that a highly accommodative monetary policy stance will be appropriate for a considerable time after the asset purchase program concludes and the economic recovery strengthens. It also reiterated its expectation that the current exceptionally low target range for the federal funds rate will be appropriate as long as the unemployment rate remains above 6-1/2 percent, projected inflation one to two years ahead is no more than a half percentage point above the 2 percent long-run goal, and long-term inflation expectations remain well anchored. The Committee anticipates that maintaining the current target range for the federal funds rate will likely be appropriate well past the time the unemployment rate falls below 6-1/2 percent, especially if projected inflation continues to be below the 2 percent long-run goal. When the Committee decides to begin removing policy accommodation, it will take a balanced approach consistent with its long-run goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation.

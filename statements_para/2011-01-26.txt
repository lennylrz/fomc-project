The Committee decided to keep its policy interest rate unchanged. It also chose to continue its program of asset acquisition as previously outlined in [month]. This involves reinvesting principal from existing securities and acquiring an additional $600 billion in longer-term Treasury bonds by the close of the second quarter of [this year]. The Committee will periodically assess the speed of these purchases and the total volume of the program, making adjustments as necessary to achieve its goals of maximum employment and price stability.

The economic recovery is ongoing, though it has not significantly improved the job market. Household spending increased late last year but is still limited by high joblessness, modest income growth, reduced housing wealth, and tight credit. Business investment in equipment and software is rising, but nonresidential construction remains weak. Employers are still hesitant to expand their workforces, and the housing sector remains depressed.

The unemployment rate is high, and underlying inflation measures are somewhat low compared to the Committee's long-term objectives. While a gradual return to higher resource utilization is expected with price stability, progress toward these goals has been slower than desired. Although commodity prices have increased, long-term inflation expectations are stable, and underlying inflation has been declining.

The Committee expects that current economic conditions, including low resource utilization, subdued inflation, and stable inflation expectations, will likely necessitate exceptionally low policy rates for an extended duration.

The Committee will continue to observe economic and financial developments, using its tools as needed to support the recovery and ensure inflation aligns with its mandate over time.

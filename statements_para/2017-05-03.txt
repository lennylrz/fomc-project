The Committee chose to keep its policy interest rate unchanged at this meeting.

Recent data indicate continued strengthening in the job market, despite a deceleration in overall economic expansion. Employment gains have been consistent over recent months, and the jobless rate has fallen. While household spending saw only modest increases, the underlying factors supporting consumer growth remain robust. Business investment in fixed assets has firmed. Inflation, measured over the past year, is currently near the Committee's 2 percent long-term target. Excluding volatile food and energy components, consumer prices decreased in [month], and inflation remained somewhat below 2 percent. Market-based gauges of inflation compensation are still low, and survey-based measures of long-term inflation expectations show little overall change.

The Committee believes the first-quarter slowdown in growth is likely temporary. It anticipates that, with gradual adjustments to monetary policy, economic activity will expand at a moderate pace, labor market conditions will strengthen further, and inflation will stabilize around 2 percent over the medium term. Near-term risks to the economic outlook appear generally balanced. The Committee continues to closely monitor inflation indicators and global economic and financial developments.

Given current and projected labor market conditions and inflation, the Committee decided to maintain its current policy rate. The monetary policy stance remains accommodative, supporting further strengthening in the labor market and a sustained return to 2 percent inflation.

Future adjustments to the policy rate will depend on an assessment of economic conditions relative to the Committee's goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. This assessment will consider a broad range of information, including labor market measures, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments. The Committee will closely watch actual and expected inflation relative to its symmetric inflation goal. The Committee expects economic conditions to warrant gradual increases in the policy rate, which is likely to remain below long-run levels for some time. However, the actual path of the policy rate will depend on the economic outlook as informed by incoming data.

The Committee will continue its current practice of reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities. This is expected to continue until the normalization of the policy rate is well underway. This approach, by maintaining substantial holdings of longer-term securities, should help keep financial conditions accommodative.

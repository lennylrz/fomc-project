The Committee chose to keep its primary interest rate unchanged. This decision reflects current and anticipated conditions in the job market and price stability. The current monetary policy approach continues to support further improvements in employment and a sustained return to the 2 percent inflation target.

Since the [month] meeting, the job market has shown continued improvement, and overall economic activity has seen moderate expansion this year. Employment gains have been consistent, and the jobless rate has fallen. Household spending and business investment have both grown. Over the past year, both overall inflation and the core measure (excluding food and energy) have decreased and are below 2 percent. Market-based indicators of inflation compensation are still low, while survey-based measures of long-term inflation expectations are largely stable.

The Committee anticipates that, with gradual adjustments to monetary policy, economic growth will continue at a moderate pace, and labor market conditions will strengthen further. Inflation is expected to stay somewhat below 2 percent in the near term but should stabilize around the 2 percent objective over the medium term. Short-term risks to the economic outlook appear generally balanced, though the Committee is closely watching inflation trends.

Future adjustments to the policy rate will depend on how economic conditions, both realized and projected, align with the goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. This evaluation will consider various data, including labor market indicators, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments. The Committee will carefully track actual and expected inflation relative to its symmetric inflation goal. The Committee expects that economic conditions will likely support gradual increases in the policy rate, which is anticipated to remain below long-run levels for some time. However, the actual path of the policy rate will be guided by incoming economic data.

For now, the Committee will continue its current practice of reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities. The Committee expects to start its balance sheet normalization program relatively soon, assuming the economy develops as expected. Details of this program are in the [month] [this year] Addendum to the Committee's Policy Normalization Principles and Plans.

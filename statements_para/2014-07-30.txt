The Committee decided to keep its policy interest rate unchanged. It also opted to decrease the monthly pace of its Treasury bond purchases by $5 billion and its mortgage-backed securities purchases by $5 billion.

Recent data indicates that economic activity expanded in the second quarter. The job market has improved, with a further drop in the unemployment rate. However, various labor market metrics suggest that significant underemployment persists. Consumer spending appears to be increasing moderately, and business investment is growing. The housing sector's recovery remains sluggish. Fiscal policy continues to constrain economic growth, though this effect is lessening. Inflation has moved somewhat closer to the Committee's 2 percent long-term objective, and long-term inflation expectations have held steady.

The Committee believes that the economy possesses sufficient underlying strength to sustain ongoing improvements in the labor market. The risks to the economic and labor market outlook are considered nearly balanced, and the probability of inflation remaining persistently below 2 percent has somewhat decreased.

The Committee will continue its asset purchases and use other policy tools until the labor market outlook has substantially improved within a stable price environment. If incoming information generally supports expectations of continued labor market improvement and inflation moving toward its long-term objective, the Committee will likely reduce the pace of asset purchases in further steps at future meetings. However, the pace of asset purchases is not predetermined and will depend on the Committee's outlook for the labor market and inflation, as well as its assessment of the effectiveness and costs of such purchases.

The Committee reaffirmed that a highly accommodative monetary policy stance remains appropriate. It will assess progress toward maximum employment and 2 percent inflation, both realized and expected, to determine how long to maintain the current policy rate. This assessment will consider labor market conditions, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial developments. The Committee anticipates that it will likely be appropriate to maintain the current policy rate for a considerable time after the asset purchase program concludes, especially if projected inflation remains below the 2 percent goal and long-term inflation expectations are stable. Even after employment and inflation are near mandate-consistent levels, economic conditions may warrant keeping the policy rate below normal long-run levels for some time. When the Committee begins to remove policy accommodation, it will take a balanced approach consistent with its long-run goals.

This member viewed such language as time-dependent and not reflective of the significant economic progress made toward the Committee's goals.

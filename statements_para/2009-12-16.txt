The Committee decided to keep its policy interest rate unchanged.

Recent data indicate continued expansion in economic activity since the [month] meeting, with a lessening of the decline in the job market. The housing sector has shown some positive developments over recent months. Household spending is growing moderately, though it faces constraints from a weak job market, limited income growth, reduced housing wealth, and tight credit availability. Businesses are still reducing fixed investment, albeit at a slower pace, and remain hesitant to hire; they are making progress in aligning inventory levels with sales. Financial conditions have become more supportive of economic growth.

While economic activity is expected to remain subdued for a period, the Committee foresees that policy measures to stabilize financial markets and institutions, along with fiscal and monetary stimulus and market forces, will contribute to stronger economic growth and a gradual return to fuller resource utilization, consistent with price stability. Given significant unused capacity likely to suppress cost pressures and stable long-term inflation expectations, the Committee anticipates that inflation will remain low for some time.

The Committee expects that economic conditions, including low resource utilization, subdued inflation trends, and stable inflation expectations, will likely necessitate exceptionally low policy rates for an extended period. To support mortgage lending and housing markets and improve overall private credit conditions, the Federal Reserve is acquiring agency mortgage-backed securities and agency debt. To ensure a smooth market transition, the Committee is gradually reducing the pace of these acquisitions, expecting completion by the end of the first quarter of [year+1]. The Committee will continue to assess the timing and total amounts of its security purchases based on the evolving economic outlook and financial market conditions.

In light of ongoing improvements in financial market functioning, the Committee and the Board of Governors anticipate that most of the Federal Reserve’s special liquidity facilities will conclude on [month] 1, [year+1], as previously announced on [month] 25, [this year]. These facilities include the Asset-Backed Commercial Paper Money Market Mutual Fund Liquidity Facility, the Commercial Paper Funding Facility, the Primary Dealer Credit Facility, and the Term Securities Lending Facility. The Federal Reserve will also work with its central bank counterparts to end its temporary liquidity swap arrangements by [month] 1. The Federal Reserve expects that amounts provided under the Term Auction Facility will continue to be reduced in early [year+1]. The anticipated expiration dates for the Term Asset-Backed Securities Loan Facility remain [month] 30, [year+1], for loans backed by new-issue commercial mortgage-backed securities and [month] 31, [year+1], for loans backed by all other collateral types. The Federal Reserve is prepared to adjust these plans if needed to support financial stability and economic growth.

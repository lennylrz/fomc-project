The Committee elected to hold its primary policy rate steady. It also resolved to continue its current rate of asset acquisitions, purchasing $40 billion in mortgage-backed securities and $45 billion in Treasury securities each month. The existing strategy of reinvesting principal from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into new mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities, will persist. These measures are intended to foster more favorable financial conditions.

Recent data indicates that the economy expanded modestly in the first half of the year. The job market has shown some improvement, though joblessness remains elevated. Household spending and business investment advanced, and the housing sector has strengthened, despite a recent uptick in mortgage rates and fiscal restraint on growth. Inflation has been below the 2 percent objective, partly due to temporary factors, but long-term inflation expectations are stable.

The Committee anticipates that with appropriate support, economic growth will accelerate, and the unemployment rate will gradually decrease towards levels consistent with its dual mandate. Risks to the economy and labor market have lessened since the fall. While persistent low inflation could pose risks, the Committee expects it to return to the 2 percent objective over the medium term.

Asset purchases and other tools will continue until there is substantial improvement in the labor market outlook within a stable price environment. The pace of purchases may be adjusted based on changes in the labor market or inflation outlook, considering their effectiveness, costs, and progress toward goals.

A highly accommodative monetary stance will be maintained for a considerable period after asset purchases conclude and the recovery strengthens. Specifically, the exceptionally low policy rate is expected to remain in place as long as the unemployment rate is above 6.5 percent, projected inflation one to two years ahead is no more than 2.5 percent, and long-term inflation expectations remain anchored. Other factors, such as labor market indicators, inflation pressures, expectations, and financial developments, will also be considered. A balanced approach will be taken when policy accommodation is eventually removed.

The Committee decided to maintain its current stance on the federal funds rate. It will also continue its existing approach to reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities. This strategy aims to preserve supportive financial conditions.

Recent data indicates a moderate expansion in economic activity. The job market has shown further improvement, with strong employment gains and a reduced unemployment rate. Overall, various labor market indicators suggest a continued reduction in underutilized labor resources. Household spending is increasing moderately, and business fixed investment is advancing, though the housing sector's recovery remains sluggish. Inflation has stayed below the Committee's long-term goal, partly due to lower energy prices. Market-based measures of inflation compensation have decreased somewhat further, while survey-based measures of long-term inflation expectations have remained stable.

The Committee anticipates that, with appropriate policy support, economic activity will grow at a moderate pace, and labor market indicators will move towards levels consistent with its dual mandate of maximum employment and price stability. The Committee views the risks to the economic and labor market outlook as nearly balanced. Inflation is expected to gradually approach 2 percent as the labor market strengthens and the temporary effects of lower energy prices and other factors fade. The Committee continues to closely monitor inflation trends.

In determining how long to maintain the current policy rate, the Committee will assess progress, both realized and anticipated, toward its objectives of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. This evaluation will consider a broad range of information, including labor market conditions, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial developments. Based on its current assessment, the Committee believes it can be patient before beginning to normalize monetary policy. This guidance aligns with its previous statement that it would likely be appropriate to maintain the current policy rate for a considerable time after the end of its asset purchase program in [month], especially if projected inflation remains below the 2 percent long-run goal and long-term inflation expectations stay well anchored. However, if incoming information suggests faster progress toward the Committee's employment and inflation objectives than currently expected, then policy rate increases may occur sooner. Conversely, if progress is slower than anticipated, then increases may occur later. Even after employment and inflation are near mandate-consistent levels, economic conditions may, for some time, warrant keeping the policy rate below levels the Committee views as normal in the longer run.

Three members dissented from this action. One member believed that while patience is warranted, the improvement in U.S. economic performance since [month] has advanced more than the majority anticipates, suggesting an earlier date for a policy rate increase. Another member felt that the decision, given ongoing low inflation and declining market-based measures of long-term inflation expectations, created undue downside risk to the credibility of the 2 percent inflation target. A third member believed the statement should not emphasize the importance of the passage of time in its forward guidance, and given improved economic conditions, should not stress consistency with previous statements.

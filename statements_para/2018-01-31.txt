The Committee chose to keep its policy interest rate unchanged at this meeting. This decision reflects the current economic situation and the anticipated path for both employment and price stability.

Recent data since the [month] gathering indicates continued strength in the job market and robust economic expansion. Employment figures, consumer spending, and business investment have all shown solid increases, and the unemployment rate has held steady at a low level. Regarding prices, both headline inflation and core inflation (excluding food and energy) have remained below the 2 percent target over the past year. While market-based indicators of future inflation have risen recently, they are still low. Survey-based measures of long-term inflation expectations have, on average, shown little change.

Looking ahead, the Committee anticipates that with further measured adjustments to monetary policy, economic growth will proceed at a moderate pace, and the labor market will stay robust. Annual inflation is projected to increase this year and settle around the Committee's 2 percent target over the medium term. The immediate risks to the economic outlook appear generally balanced, though the Committee is closely observing inflation trends.

The current accommodative monetary policy stance is seen as supportive of a strong labor market and a sustained return to 2 percent inflation.

Future adjustments to the policy rate will depend on how economic conditions, both realized and projected, align with the Committee's goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. This evaluation will consider various data points, including labor market metrics, indicators of price pressures and inflation expectations, and developments in financial markets and internationally. The Committee will carefully track actual and expected inflation relative to its symmetric inflation objective. The Committee expects that economic conditions will likely necessitate further gradual increases in the policy rate, which is anticipated to remain below its long-run equilibrium for some time. However, the actual trajectory of the policy rate will be guided by incoming economic data and the evolving outlook.

The Committee chose to keep its policy interest rate unchanged at this gathering.

Recent data indicate that economic expansion has been moderate, despite global economic and financial events in recent months. Household spending has grown moderately, and the housing sector has improved. However, business fixed investment and net exports have been weak. Various recent indicators, including strong job creation, suggest continued strengthening of the labor market. Inflation has increased recently but remains below the 2 percent long-term objective, partly due to lower energy and non-energy import prices. Market-based inflation compensation measures are still low, while survey-based long-term inflation expectations have shown little overall change recently.

The Committee anticipates that, with gradual adjustments to monetary policy, economic activity will expand moderately, and labor market indicators will continue to strengthen. Nevertheless, global economic and financial developments still present risks. Inflation is expected to remain low in the near term, partly due to past energy price declines, but should rise to 2 percent over the medium term as temporary effects of lower energy and import prices fade and the labor market improves further. The Committee is closely monitoring inflation trends.

Future adjustments to the policy rate will depend on an assessment of economic conditions relative to maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. This evaluation will consider labor market measures, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments. Given the current inflation shortfall from 2 percent, the Committee will carefully track progress toward its inflation goal. Economic conditions are expected to warrant only gradual increases in the policy rate, which will likely remain below long-run levels for some time. However, the actual path will depend on the economic outlook informed by incoming data.

The Committee will continue its current practice of reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities and rolling over maturing Treasury securities. This is expected to continue until the normalization of the policy rate is well advanced, thereby maintaining accommodative financial conditions.

The Committee has opted to increase its primary policy interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point.

This adjustment leaves the monetary policy stance still supportive, which, alongside strong productivity gains, continues to bolster economic activity. Recent information suggests that output growth remains robust, and the employment situation has improved. While recent inflation figures are somewhat high, a portion of this rise seems attributable to temporary influences.

The Committee views the potential for both stronger and weaker outcomes for sustained growth and stable prices over the coming quarters as roughly balanced. Given that underlying inflation is still anticipated to be quite low, the Committee expects that policy adjustments can proceed at a gradual pace. However, the Committee stands ready to alter its approach if economic conditions shift, to uphold its commitment to price stability.

Separately, the Board of Governors endorsed a quarter percentage point rise in the discount rate. This decision followed requests from the Boards of Directors of numerous Federal Reserve Banks.

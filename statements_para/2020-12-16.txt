The Committee has opted to maintain its primary interest rate at its current level.

Economic activity and employment have shown continued improvement, though they remain considerably below their pre-[year] levels. Consumer price increases have been subdued due to reduced demand and earlier drops in oil prices. Financial conditions generally remain supportive, partly due to policy actions aimed at bolstering the economy and credit availability for households and businesses. The [event] pandemic continues to inflict significant human and economic hardship globally and domestically.

The trajectory of the economy is heavily reliant on the progression of the virus. The ongoing public health crisis will continue to suppress economic activity, employment, and inflation in the near term, and presents substantial dangers to the economic outlook over the medium term.

The Committee aims for maximum employment and a 2 percent inflation rate over the long run. Given that inflation has consistently been below this long-term target, the Committee intends to allow inflation to rise moderately above 2 percent for a period, ensuring that inflation averages 2 percent over time and that long-term inflation expectations remain firmly anchored at 2 percent. The Committee anticipates keeping monetary policy accommodative until these objectives are met. The Committee expects to maintain its policy rate until labor market conditions align with its maximum employment assessments and inflation reaches 2 percent and is on track to moderately surpass 2 percent for some time. Furthermore, the Federal Reserve will continue to expand its holdings of Treasury securities by at least $80 billion each month and agency mortgage-backed securities by at least $40 billion each month until significant additional progress has been made toward its maximum employment and price stability goals. These asset acquisitions contribute to smooth market operations and accommodative financial conditions, thereby facilitating credit flow to households and businesses.

In evaluating the appropriate monetary policy stance, the Committee will persist in monitoring the implications of new information for the economic outlook. The Committee stands ready to adjust the monetary policy stance as necessary if risks arise that could hinder the achievement of its goals. The Committee's evaluations will consider a broad spectrum of information, including public health data, labor market conditions, inflationary pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments.

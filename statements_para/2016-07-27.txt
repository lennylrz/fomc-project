Overall, employment figures and other labor market metrics suggest an increase in labor utilization recently. Household spending has shown strong growth, though business fixed investment has been soft. Inflation remains below the Committee's 2 percent long-term target, partly due to earlier drops in energy and non-energy import prices. Market-based inflation compensation measures are still low, while most survey-based long-term inflation expectations have been largely stable in recent months.

The Committee anticipates that, with gradual adjustments to monetary policy, economic activity will expand moderately and labor market indicators will improve. Inflation is expected to stay low in the near term, partly due to past energy price declines, but should rise to 2 percent over the medium term as temporary effects of past energy and import price drops fade and the labor market strengthens further. Short-term risks to the economic outlook have lessened. The Committee continues to closely observe inflation indicators and global economic and financial developments.

The current monetary policy stance remains supportive, aiding further improvements in labor market conditions and a return to 2 percent inflation. The Committee will continue its existing practice of reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities. This is expected to continue until the normalization of the federal funds rate is well advanced, helping to maintain accommodative financial conditions.

Future adjustments to the federal funds rate target will depend on an assessment of economic conditions relative to maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. This evaluation will consider various data, including labor market conditions, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments. Given the current inflation shortfall, the Committee will carefully monitor progress toward its inflation goal. Economic conditions are expected to warrant only gradual increases in the federal funds rate, which is likely to remain below its long-run equilibrium for some time. However, the actual path will be determined by incoming data and the economic outlook.

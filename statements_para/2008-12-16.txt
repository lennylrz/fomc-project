Today, the Committee reduced its primary policy interest rate by three-quarters of a percentage point.

Since the prior meeting, the employment situation has worsened, and incoming information shows reductions in household spending, corporate investment, and factory output. Financial markets continue to experience considerable stress, and credit availability is restricted. The economic outlook has deteriorated further.

Concurrently, price pressures have significantly lessened. Given the fall in energy and other commodity prices, alongside the weaker economic prospects, the Committee anticipates further moderation in inflation over the coming quarters.

The Federal Reserve will utilize all available instruments to foster a return to sustained economic expansion and maintain stable prices. Specifically, the Committee foresees that subdued economic conditions will likely necessitate exceptionally low levels of the federal funds rate for an extended period.

The Committee's future policy will concentrate on bolstering financial market operations and stimulating the economy via open market activities and other measures that keep the Federal Reserve's balance sheet substantial. As previously announced, over the next few quarters, the Federal Reserve will acquire significant amounts of agency debt and mortgage-backed securities to aid the mortgage and housing sectors, and it is prepared to increase these purchases if conditions require. The Committee is also assessing the advantages of buying longer-term Treasury securities. Early next year, the Federal Reserve will also launch the Term Asset-Backed Securities Loan Facility to facilitate lending to households and small businesses. The Federal Reserve will continue to explore methods of employing its balance sheet to further support credit markets and economic activity.

In a related move, the Board of Governors unanimously approved a 75-basis-point reduction in the discount rate.

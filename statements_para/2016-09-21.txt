The Committee chose to keep its policy interest rate unchanged. While the argument for an increase has grown stronger, the Committee opted to await additional confirmation of ongoing advancement toward its goals.

Economic activity has accelerated from the modest pace observed earlier this year, and the job market continues to improve. Although the unemployment rate has shown little change recently, job creation has been consistently robust. Household spending has been vigorous, but business fixed investment remains subdued. Inflation continues to be below the 2 percent long-term target, partly due to earlier decreases in energy and non-energy import prices. Market-based inflation compensation measures are still low, and most survey-based long-term inflation expectations have been largely stable.

The Committee anticipates that, with gradual adjustments to monetary policy, the economy will expand moderately, and labor market conditions will strengthen further. Inflation is expected to stay low in the near term, partly due to past energy price declines, but should rise to 2 percent over the medium term as temporary effects of earlier price drops fade and the labor market tightens. Near-term risks to the economic outlook appear generally balanced. The Committee will continue to closely watch inflation indicators and global economic and financial developments.

Monetary policy remains supportive, fostering further labor market improvement and a return to 2 percent inflation. Future adjustments to the policy rate will depend on an assessment of economic conditions relative to maximum employment and 2 percent inflation, considering labor market data, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments. Given the current inflation shortfall, the Committee will carefully monitor progress toward its inflation goal. Economic conditions are expected to warrant only gradual increases in the policy rate, which is likely to remain below long-run levels for some time. However, the actual path will depend on incoming data.

The Committee will continue its current practice of reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities and rolling over maturing Treasury securities until the normalization of the policy rate is well advanced. This approach helps maintain accommodative financial conditions by keeping substantial holdings of longer-term securities.

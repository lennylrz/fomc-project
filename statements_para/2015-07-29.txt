The Committee decided to maintain its policy interest rate at its current setting. It also chose to continue its existing approach of reinvesting principal from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities. This strategy aims to sustain supportive financial conditions by keeping the central bank's holdings of long-term assets substantial.

Recent data, since the [month] meeting, indicate a moderate expansion in economic activity. Household spending has grown moderately, and the housing sector has shown further improvement. However, business fixed investment and net exports remained weak. The job market continued to strengthen, marked by solid job creation and a decrease in unemployment. Overall, various labor market indicators suggest that the underutilization of labor resources has lessened since early this year. Inflation remained below the Committee's long-term goal, partly due to earlier drops in energy prices and declining non-energy import costs. Market-based gauges of inflation compensation are still low, while survey-based measures of long-term inflation expectations have been stable.

The Committee expects economic activity to expand at a moderate pace with appropriate policy support, and labor market indicators to continue moving toward levels consistent with its dual mandate. The risks to the outlook for economic activity and the labor market are considered nearly balanced. Inflation is projected to stay near its recent low level in the near term but is anticipated to gradually rise toward 2 percent over the medium term as the labor market improves further and the temporary effects of earlier declines in energy and import prices fade. The Committee is closely monitoring inflation developments.

The Committee anticipates that an increase in the policy rate will be appropriate once it observes further improvement in the labor market and is reasonably confident that inflation will return to its 2 percent objective over the medium term. When the Committee eventually begins to withdraw policy accommodation, it will adopt a balanced approach consistent with its long-term goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. Even after employment and inflation are near mandate-consistent levels, economic conditions may, for some time, justify keeping the policy rate below levels considered normal in the longer run.

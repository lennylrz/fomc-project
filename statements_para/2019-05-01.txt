The Committee chose to keep its primary policy rate unchanged.

Recent data indicates a robust employment situation and healthy economic expansion. The job market has shown consistent strength recently, with unemployment staying at a low level. However, consumer spending and business investment saw a deceleration in the first three months of the year. Over the past year, both headline and core inflation measures have decreased and are currently below the 2 percent target. Market-based indicators of future inflation have generally stayed low, and surveys of long-term inflation expectations show little change.

The Committee anticipates that continued economic growth, a strong labor market, and inflation approaching its symmetric 2 percent goal are the most probable future developments. Given global economic and financial trends, alongside subdued inflationary pressures, the Committee will proceed cautiously in deciding on any future adjustments to its policy rate to achieve these objectives.

When considering the timing and magnitude of future policy rate changes, the Committee will evaluate current and projected economic conditions against its goals of maximum employment and symmetric 2 percent inflation. This evaluation will incorporate various data points, including labor market indicators, measures of inflationary pressures and expectations, and developments in financial markets and the international economy.

The Committee chose to keep its primary interest rate unchanged at this gathering. This decision provides an opportunity to evaluate new data and its impact on monetary policy.

Economic activity has shown continued modest growth. The job market has seen strong gains recently, and the jobless rate is still low. Price increases are still high. The nation's banking sector is robust. It is anticipated that stricter lending conditions for consumers and businesses will dampen economic activity, employment, and inflation, though the full impact is not yet clear. The Committee is very focused on the dangers posed by inflation.

To achieve its objectives of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation over the long term, the Committee will continue to decrease its holdings of government bonds and mortgage-backed securities as previously outlined. When considering if further policy tightening is needed to bring inflation back to 2 percent, the Committee will consider the total amount of policy tightening already implemented, the delayed effects of monetary policy on the economy and inflation, and current economic and financial conditions. The Committee is firmly dedicated to reaching its 2 percent inflation target.

The Committee will keep an eye on how new information affects the economic outlook when deciding on the right monetary policy stance. Should risks arise that could hinder the Committee's goals, it stands ready to adjust policy as needed. These evaluations will incorporate a broad range of data, including labor market indicators, inflationary pressures and expectations, and global financial developments.

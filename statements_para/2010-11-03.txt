The Committee decided to keep its policy interest rate unchanged. It also chose to increase its holdings of securities. The existing approach of reinvesting principal payments from its securities portfolio will continue. Furthermore, the Committee plans to acquire an additional $600 billion in longer-term Treasury securities by the close of the second quarter of [year+1], equating to roughly $75 billion each month. The Committee will routinely assess the speed of these purchases and the total size of the program based on new information, making adjustments as necessary to best support maximum employment and price stability.

Recent data since the [month] meeting indicate that the economic recovery and job growth remain sluggish. Household spending is gradually rising but is held back by high unemployment, modest income gains, reduced housing wealth, and tight credit conditions. Business investment in equipment and software is increasing, though at a slower pace than earlier in the year, and investment in nonresidential structures remains weak. Employers are still hesitant to expand their workforces. Housing starts continue to be depressed. While long-term inflation expectations have held steady, underlying inflation measures have declined in recent quarters.

The unemployment rate is currently elevated, and underlying inflation is somewhat low compared to the Committee's long-run objectives for maximum employment and price stability. Although a gradual return to higher resource utilization and price stability is expected, progress toward these goals has been disappointingly slow.

The Committee anticipates that economic conditions, including low resource utilization, subdued inflation, and stable inflation expectations, will likely necessitate exceptionally low policy rates for an extended period.

The Committee will continue to observe economic and financial developments, using its policy instruments as needed to support the recovery and ensure inflation aligns with its mandate over time.

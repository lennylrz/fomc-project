Today, the Committee decided to maintain its primary policy interest rate at its current level.

Regarding the economic landscape, recent data since [month] indicate a continued moderate expansion of economic activity. Job creation has been sluggish, and the unemployment rate remains elevated. Consumer spending has advanced, but business investment in fixed assets appears to have slowed. The housing market shows some signs of improvement, though from a low base. Inflation has been subdued, despite recent increases in some commodity prices. Long-term inflation expectations have remained stable.

The Committee is concerned that, without further support, economic growth may not be sufficient to achieve sustained improvement in the labor market. Furthermore, global financial market stresses continue to pose significant risks to the economic outlook. The Committee also anticipates that inflation over the medium term will likely be at or below its 2 percent objective.

To foster a stronger recovery and ensure inflation aligns with its dual mandate over time, the Committee will increase its holdings of agency mortgage-backed securities by $40 billion per month. It will also continue its program to extend the average maturity of its securities holdings through the end of the year, as announced in [month]. Additionally, the Committee will maintain its existing practice of reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities. These actions, which will increase the Committee’s longer-term securities holdings by approximately $85 billion monthly through the end of the year, are intended to lower long-term interest rates, support mortgage markets, and generally ease financial conditions.

The Committee will closely monitor economic and financial developments in the coming months. If the labor market outlook does not improve substantially, the Committee will continue its purchases of agency mortgage-backed securities, undertake additional asset purchases, and utilize other policy tools as needed, all within a context of price stability. In determining the scale, pace, and composition of these purchases, the Committee will consider their likely effectiveness and costs.

To support progress toward maximum employment and price stability, the Committee expects that a highly accommodative monetary policy stance will be appropriate for a considerable period after the economic recovery strengthens. The Committee anticipates that exceptionally low levels for the federal funds rate will likely be warranted at least through mid-[year+3].

The Committee has opted to decrease its primary policy rate by a quarter of a percentage point. Concurrently, the Board of Governors sanctioned a similar 25 basis point reduction in the discount rate.

The Committee maintains that its current monetary posture, alongside strong underlying productivity gains, continues to bolster economic activity. Recent data indicate a strengthening in expenditures, significantly better financial conditions, and a stabilization in both employment and goods markets. However, the economy has not yet demonstrated consistent expansion. Given contained inflation expectations, the Committee concluded that a slightly more expansive monetary approach would further aid an economy anticipated to improve over time.

The Committee assesses that the potential for stronger or weaker sustainable growth in the coming quarters is evenly balanced. Nevertheless, the slight chance of an undesirable, significant drop in inflation is greater than the chance of inflation rising from its current low level. Overall, the Committee expects this latter concern to be more prominent in the near future.

One member, [member], dissented from this decision, advocating for a larger 50 basis point reduction in the policy rate.

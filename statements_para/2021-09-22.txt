The Committee has chosen to maintain its current policy rate without alteration.

Economic activity and employment indicators have shown continued improvement, supported by vaccination efforts and robust policy measures. Sectors previously hit hardest by the pandemic have seen recent gains, though the increase in [event] cases has somewhat slowed their recovery. Inflation is currently elevated, primarily due to temporary factors. Financial conditions generally remain supportive, partly due to policies designed to aid the economy and credit flow to households and businesses.

The economic trajectory remains dependent on the virus's progression. Further vaccination progress is anticipated to lessen the public health crisis's economic impact, but the economic outlook still faces risks.

The Committee aims for maximum employment and a 2 percent inflation rate over the long term. Given persistent inflation below this target, the Committee intends to achieve inflation moderately above 2 percent for a period, ensuring a 2 percent average over time and stable long-term inflation expectations at 2 percent. An accommodative monetary policy stance will be maintained until these objectives are met. The Committee expects to keep the policy rate at its current level until labor market conditions align with maximum employment assessments and inflation reaches 2 percent, on track to moderately surpass it for some time. Since [month], when the Committee stated it would continue increasing its Treasury and mortgage-backed securities holdings by at least $80 billion and $40 billion monthly, respectively, until substantial further progress toward its goals, the economy has advanced. If this progress continues as broadly expected, a reduction in the pace of asset purchases may soon be appropriate. These purchases facilitate smooth market operations and accommodative financial conditions, thereby supporting credit availability for households and businesses.

In determining the appropriate policy stance, the Committee will continue to evaluate incoming data's implications for the economic outlook. Should risks arise that could hinder the Committee's goals, it stands ready to adjust monetary policy as needed. Assessments will incorporate a broad spectrum of information, including public health data, labor market conditions, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments.

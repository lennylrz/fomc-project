The Committee chose to keep its policy interest rate unchanged.

Recent data indicates continued strengthening in the job market, with economic activity showing an improved pace compared to the first half of the year. While the unemployment rate has seen little change recently, job creation has been robust. Household spending has shown moderate growth, though business fixed investment remains subdued. Inflation has risen somewhat this year but is still below the Committee's 2 percent long-term target, partly due to earlier declines in energy and non-energy import prices. Market-based inflation compensation measures have increased but are still low; most survey-based long-term inflation expectations have been largely stable recently.

The Committee anticipates that, with measured adjustments to monetary policy, the economy will expand moderately, and labor market conditions will improve further. Inflation is projected to reach 2 percent over the medium term as temporary effects from past energy and import price declines fade and the labor market strengthens. Near-term risks to the economic outlook appear generally balanced. The Committee continues to closely observe inflation indicators and global economic and financial developments.

The Committee believes the argument for raising the policy rate has grown stronger but decided to await additional evidence of progress toward its goals for now. The current monetary policy stance remains supportive, aiding further labor market improvement and a return to 2 percent inflation.

Future adjustments to the policy rate will depend on an assessment of economic conditions against maximum employment and 2 percent inflation objectives. This evaluation will consider various data, including labor market indicators, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments. Given the current inflation shortfall, the Committee will carefully monitor progress toward its inflation goal. Economic conditions are expected to warrant only gradual increases in the policy rate, which is likely to remain below long-run levels for some time. However, the actual path will be guided by incoming data.

The Committee will continue its current practice of reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities and rolling over maturing Treasury securities until the normalization of the policy rate is well advanced. This approach helps maintain accommodative financial conditions by keeping substantial holdings of longer-term securities.

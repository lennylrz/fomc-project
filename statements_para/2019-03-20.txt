The Committee chose to keep its policy rate unchanged.

Recent data indicates that economic expansion has decelerated from its robust pace observed in the final quarter of last year, although the employment situation remains strong. While payroll figures showed little movement in [month], job creation has been consistently healthy over recent periods, and the jobless rate has stayed low. Indicators from the first quarter suggest a slowdown in consumer spending and business capital expenditures. Over the past year, overall price increases have moderated, primarily due to reduced energy costs; inflation excluding food and energy items is still close to 2 percent. Market-based gauges of inflation compensation have generally stayed low recently, and survey-based measures of long-term inflation expectations show little change.

The Committee anticipates that continued economic growth, a robust job market, and inflation around its symmetric 2 percent target are the most probable scenarios. Given global economic and financial trends, alongside subdued inflationary pressures, the Committee will proceed cautiously in deciding on any future modifications to the policy rate needed to support these outcomes.

When considering the timing and magnitude of future policy rate adjustments, the Committee will evaluate current and projected economic conditions against its goals of maximum employment and symmetric 2 percent inflation. This evaluation will incorporate a broad spectrum of information, including labor market metrics, indicators of inflationary pressures and expectations, and developments in financial markets and internationally.

The Committee decided to keep its policy interest rate unchanged. It also concluded its asset purchase program this month. The existing approach of reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities, will continue. This strategy aims to sustain supportive financial conditions by maintaining substantial holdings of longer-term securities.

Recent data since [month] indicate that economic activity is expanding at a moderate pace. The job market has shown further improvement, with solid employment gains and a lower unemployment rate. Overall, various labor market indicators suggest a gradual reduction in underutilized labor resources. Household spending is increasing moderately, and business fixed investment is advancing, though the housing sector's recovery remains slow. Inflation continues to be below the Committee's long-term objective. Market-based inflation compensation measures have decreased slightly, while survey-based long-term inflation expectations have held steady.

The Committee expects that, with appropriate policy support, economic activity will grow moderately, and both labor market indicators and inflation will move toward levels consistent with its dual mandate. The risks to the economic activity and labor market outlook are considered nearly balanced. While lower energy prices and other factors will likely depress near-term inflation, the Committee believes the probability of inflation persistently remaining below 2 percent has somewhat decreased since early this year.

The Committee judges that the labor market outlook has significantly improved since the start of its current asset purchase program. Furthermore, the Committee continues to perceive sufficient underlying strength in the broader economy to support ongoing progress toward maximum employment within a stable price environment.

To support continued progress toward maximum employment and price stability, the Committee reaffirmed its view that the current policy rate range remains appropriate. In deciding how long to maintain this range, the Committee will evaluate both current and projected progress toward its goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. This evaluation will consider a broad array of information, including labor market conditions, inflation pressures, inflation expectations, and financial developments. The Committee anticipates that, based on its current assessment, it will likely be appropriate to maintain the current policy rate range for a considerable period after the asset purchase program ends this month, particularly if projected inflation remains below the Committee's 2 percent long-run goal and long-term inflation expectations stay well anchored. However, if incoming information shows faster progress toward the Committee's employment and inflation objectives than currently expected, policy rate increases could occur sooner. Conversely, if progress is slower than anticipated, policy rate increases could occur later.

When the Committee decides to begin removing policy accommodation, it will adopt a balanced approach consistent with its long-run goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. The Committee currently expects that, even when employment and inflation are near mandate-consistent levels, economic conditions may, for some time, warrant keeping the policy rate below levels the Committee views as normal in the longer run.

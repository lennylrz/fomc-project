The Committee has opted to increase its primary policy interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point.

This adjustment occurs as the Committee assesses that the current monetary policy stance continues to be supportive of economic activity, especially when considering the strong underlying growth in productivity. Recent economic indicators suggest a slight moderation in the pace of spending growth, which is partly attributed to previous increases in energy costs. Despite this, the labor market appears to be experiencing continued gradual improvement. Inflationary pressures have intensified in recent months, and businesses are showing greater ability to set prices. Expectations for long-term inflation, however, remain stable.

Looking ahead, the Committee anticipates that, with suitable monetary policy interventions, the potential for both stronger and weaker outcomes for sustained growth and price stability can be balanced. Given that underlying inflation is projected to remain under control, the Committee believes that the process of withdrawing policy support can proceed at a gradual pace. Nevertheless, the Committee stands ready to adjust its approach based on evolving economic conditions to meet its commitment to maintaining price stability.

Concurrently, the Board of Governors unanimously endorsed a quarter-percentage-point increase in the discount rate. This decision followed the approval of requests from the Boards of Directors of the Federal Reserve Banks of Boston, New York, Philadelphia, Cleveland, Richmond, Atlanta, Chicago, St. Louis, Minneapolis, Kansas City, Dallas, and San Francisco.

The Committee decided to keep its policy interest rate unchanged. It also maintained its current approach to reinvesting principal payments from its holdings of agency debt and mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities. This strategy aims to sustain supportive financial conditions.

Recent data indicates that economic expansion has been robust. The job market has shown further improvement, marked by significant job creation and a lower unemployment rate. Overall, various labor market indicators suggest that the underutilization of labor resources continues to lessen. Household spending is increasing moderately, aided by recent reductions in energy prices that have boosted purchasing power. Business fixed investment is advancing, though the housing sector's recovery remains gradual. Inflation has fallen further below the Committee's long-term goal, primarily due to declining energy costs. Market-based measures of expected inflation have decreased considerably in recent months, while survey-based measures of long-term inflation expectations have held steady.

The Committee anticipates that, with appropriate policy support, economic activity will expand at a moderate pace. Labor market indicators are expected to continue moving toward levels consistent with its dual mandate of maximum employment and price stability. The Committee continues to view the risks to the economic activity and labor market outlook as nearly balanced. Inflation is projected to decline further in the near term but is expected to gradually rise toward 2 percent over the medium term as the labor market strengthens and the temporary effects of lower energy prices and other factors fade. The Committee is closely monitoring inflation developments.

The Committee judges that it can be patient in beginning to normalize monetary policy. However, if incoming information shows faster progress toward its employment and inflation objectives than currently expected, then increases in the policy rate are likely to occur sooner. Conversely, if progress is slower than anticipated, then increases are likely to occur later. When the Committee decides to begin removing policy accommodation, it will take a balanced approach consistent with its long-run goals. The Committee currently expects that, even after employment and inflation are near mandate-consistent levels, economic conditions may, for some time, warrant keeping the policy rate below levels considered normal in the longer run.

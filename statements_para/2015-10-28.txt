The Committee decided to maintain its policy interest rate at its current level.

Recent data indicate a moderate expansion of economic activity. Household spending and business investment have shown solid increases recently, and the housing sector has improved further, though net exports have been weak. Job growth has slowed, and the unemployment rate remained stable. Nevertheless, overall labor market indicators suggest a reduction in labor underutilization since early this year. Inflation remains below the Committee's long-term goal, partly due to lower energy and non-energy import prices. Market-based inflation expectations have slightly decreased, while survey-based long-term expectations are stable.

The Committee expects moderate economic growth and continued progress in labor market indicators toward levels consistent with its dual mandate, assuming appropriate policy accommodation. Risks to the economic and labor market outlook are considered nearly balanced, but global economic and financial developments are being monitored. Inflation is projected to stay low in the near term but is expected to gradually approach 2 percent over the medium term as the labor market strengthens and the temporary effects of lower energy and import prices fade. Inflation developments are being closely watched.

To support progress toward maximum employment and price stability, the Committee reaffirmed its view that the current policy rate range is appropriate. Future adjustments to the policy rate will depend on observed and anticipated progress toward maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. This assessment will consider various factors, including labor market conditions, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments. The Committee anticipates that a policy rate increase will be appropriate once further labor market improvement is evident and there is reasonable confidence that inflation will return to its 2 percent objective over the medium term.

The Committee will continue its current practice of reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities. This approach helps maintain accommodative financial conditions by keeping substantial holdings of longer-term securities.

When policy accommodation is eventually removed, the Committee will adopt a balanced strategy consistent with its long-term goals. Even when employment and inflation are near mandate-consistent levels, economic conditions may warrant keeping the policy rate below normal long-run levels for some time.

One member, [member], dissented, preferring an increase of twenty-five basis points in the policy rate at this meeting.

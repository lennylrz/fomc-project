The Committee has opted to increase its primary policy rate by a quarter of a percentage point.

The deceleration in economic output during the final quarter of [year-1] appears to have been primarily due to transient or specific influences. Economic expansion has shown a robust recovery in the current quarter, though it is anticipated to settle into a more enduring rhythm. To date, the surge in energy and other commodity prices has had only a limited impact on underlying inflation. Continued improvements in productivity have helped to restrain the rise in labor costs per unit, and expectations for future price increases remain stable. Nevertheless, potential increases in the utilization of economic resources, alongside the elevated costs of energy and other commodities, could contribute to inflationary pressures.

The Committee believes that additional tightening of monetary policy might be necessary to maintain an approximate equilibrium in the risks to achieving both sustained economic expansion and stable prices. Regardless, the Committee will adjust its approach in response to shifts in economic forecasts as required to advance these goals.

In a related move, the Board of Governors sanctioned a 25-basis-point rise in the discount rate. This decision followed the approval of requests from the Boards of Directors of the Federal Reserve Banks of Boston, New York, Philadelphia, Cleveland, Richmond, Atlanta, Chicago, St. Louis, Minneapolis, Dallas, and San Francisco.

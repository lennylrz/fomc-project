The Committee decided to increase its policy interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point.

Economic data indicate a modest expansion in consumption and output. Employment growth has accelerated recently and is strong; the jobless rate has stayed low. Price increases are still high. The American banking system is robust and stable. Recent events will probably lead to stricter lending conditions for consumers and businesses, which will likely dampen economic activity, job creation, and inflation. The full impact of these effects is not yet clear. The Committee remains very focused on the dangers of inflation.

The Committee aims for maximum employment and a 2 percent inflation rate over the long term. To achieve these objectives, the Committee anticipates that further policy tightening might be necessary to reach a sufficiently restrictive stance to bring inflation back to 2 percent eventually. When deciding on the magnitude of future rate increases, the Committee will consider the total amount of monetary policy tightening already implemented, the delayed effects of monetary policy on the economy and inflation, and evolving economic and financial conditions. Furthermore, the Committee will continue to reduce its holdings of government bonds and agency debt and mortgage-backed securities, as previously outlined. The Committee is firmly dedicated to achieving its 2 percent inflation target.

In evaluating the proper monetary policy stance, the Committee will keep observing how new information affects the economic outlook. The Committee stands ready to modify its policy stance if risks arise that could hinder the achievement of its goals. The Committee's evaluations will incorporate a broad array of information, including labor market indicators, inflationary pressures and expectations, and financial and global developments.

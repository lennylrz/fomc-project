The Committee chose to keep its primary interest rate unchanged at this meeting. It also affirmed its ongoing strategy of reinvesting principal from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and of rolling over maturing Treasury bonds. This approach is expected to continue until the federal funds rate's normalization process is well advanced, thereby sustaining supportive financial conditions through substantial holdings of longer-term assets.

Recent data since [month] indicate continued strengthening in the job market and moderate economic expansion. Employment gains have been solid, and the jobless rate remains near its recent low. Household spending has seen moderate increases, though business fixed investment remains weak. Consumer and business confidence have recently improved. While inflation has risen in recent quarters, it still falls short of the Committee's 2 percent long-term target. Market-based inflation compensation measures are still low, and most survey-based long-term inflation expectations show little overall change.

The Committee anticipates that, with gradual adjustments to monetary policy, economic activity will expand moderately, labor market conditions will improve somewhat further, and inflation will reach 2 percent over the medium term. The near-term economic outlook faces roughly balanced risks. The Committee continues to closely watch inflation indicators and global economic and financial developments.

Future adjustments to the policy rate will depend on an assessment of economic conditions against the goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. This evaluation will consider various data, including labor market measures, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments. Given the current inflation shortfall from 2 percent, the Committee will carefully track progress toward its inflation objective. Economic conditions are expected to evolve in a way that supports only gradual increases in the federal funds rate, which is likely to stay below longer-run expected levels for some time. However, the actual path of the federal funds rate will be guided by incoming economic data.

The Committee chose to maintain its primary interest rate at its current level.

Economic activity and job creation have been less robust than anticipated, even considering that these figures largely reflect conditions prior to the recent cessation of conflict. Nevertheless, the reduction in global political instability has led to lower energy costs, improved consumer sentiment, and stronger financial markets for both bonds and stocks. These factors, combined with supportive monetary policy and sustained gains in efficiency, are expected to contribute to a gradual economic recovery.

While the precise timing and magnitude of this recovery are unclear, the Committee judges that, in the coming quarters, the potential for stronger-than-expected growth is roughly balanced with the potential for weaker-than-expected growth. However, during this same timeframe, the likelihood of an undesirable significant decline in the inflation rate, though small, is greater than the chance of inflation rising from its already subdued level. Overall, the Committee assesses that the risks to achieving its objectives are tilted towards economic softness for the foreseeable future.

The Committee decided to maintain its current policy rate.

Economic activity and employment have improved, supported by vaccinations and policy measures. However, sectors most impacted by the [event] pandemic are still weak, though showing some recovery. Inflation has increased, primarily due to temporary factors. Financial conditions remain supportive, partly due to policies aimed at aiding the economy and credit flow to households and businesses.

The economic trajectory hinges significantly on the virus's progression, including vaccination efforts. The ongoing public health crisis continues to burden the economy, and uncertainties persist regarding the economic outlook.

The Committee aims for maximum employment and a 2 percent inflation rate over the long term. Given that inflation has consistently been below this target, the Committee intends to allow inflation to moderately surpass 2 percent for a period, ensuring a 2 percent average over time and stable long-term inflation expectations at 2 percent. An accommodative monetary policy stance will be maintained until these objectives are met. The Committee anticipates keeping its policy rate unchanged until labor market conditions align with its maximum employment assessment and inflation reaches 2 percent, on track to moderately exceed it for some time. Furthermore, the Federal Reserve will continue its monthly purchases of Treasury securities by at least $80 billion and agency mortgage-backed securities by at least $40 billion until substantial further progress toward maximum employment and price stability is achieved. These asset purchases contribute to smooth market operations and supportive financial conditions, facilitating credit access for households and businesses.

The Committee will continue to evaluate incoming data for its implications on the economic outlook when determining the appropriate policy stance. Should risks arise that could hinder the achievement of its goals, the Committee stands ready to adjust monetary policy as needed. Its evaluations will consider a broad spectrum of information, including public health data, labor market conditions, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments.

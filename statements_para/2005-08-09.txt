Today, the Committee increased its primary policy interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point.

Even after this adjustment, the Committee judges that the current monetary policy stance continues to support economic expansion, especially when combined with strong underlying productivity gains. Overall spending has shown increased vigor since late winter, notwithstanding elevated energy costs. The employment situation is also steadily improving. While core inflation has been modest recently and long-term inflation expectations are stable, inflationary pressures persist at an elevated level.

The Committee anticipates that, with suitable monetary policy, the potential for both stronger and weaker outcomes for sustained growth and stable prices should remain balanced. Given the expectation of controlled underlying inflation, the Committee believes that the removal of monetary accommodation can proceed at a deliberate pace. However, the Committee stands ready to adjust its approach based on evolving economic conditions to meet its commitment to price stability.

Separately, the Board of Governors unanimously endorsed a 25-basis-point rise in the discount rate. This action followed requests from the Boards of Directors of several Federal Reserve Banks.

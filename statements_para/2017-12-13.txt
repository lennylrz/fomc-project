The Committee decided to increase its policy interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point.

Recent data indicate continued strengthening in the job market and a solid pace of economic expansion. Employment gains have been robust, even accounting for hurricane impacts, and the unemployment rate has fallen further. Household spending has grown moderately, and business fixed investment has accelerated in recent quarters. Over the past year, both overall and core inflation have decreased and are currently below the 2 percent target. Market-based inflation compensation measures remain low, while survey-based longer-term inflation expectations are largely unchanged.

The Committee's mandate is to achieve maximum employment and price stability. While recent hurricanes have affected economic activity, employment, and inflation, they have not significantly altered the national economic outlook. The Committee anticipates that, with gradual adjustments to monetary policy, economic activity will continue to grow moderately, and labor market strength will persist. Inflation is expected to stay somewhat below 2 percent in the near term but to reach the Committee's 2 percent objective over the medium term. Near-term economic risks appear balanced, but the Committee is closely watching inflation trends. The current monetary policy stance remains supportive of a strong labor market and a sustained return to 2 percent inflation.

Future adjustments to the policy rate will depend on the Committee's assessment of economic conditions against its goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. This assessment will consider various data, including labor market indicators, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments. The Committee will carefully monitor inflation relative to its symmetric goal. Economic conditions are expected to warrant gradual policy rate increases, and the rate will likely remain below its long-run equilibrium for some time. However, the actual path will be determined by incoming data and the evolving economic outlook.

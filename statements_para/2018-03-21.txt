The Committee decided to increase its policy interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point.

Recent data indicate continued strengthening in the job market and a moderate expansion of economic activity. Employment gains have been robust recently, and the unemployment rate has remained low. Household spending and business investment growth have slowed from their strong performance in the fourth quarter. Over the past year, both headline and core inflation have stayed below the 2 percent target. Market-based indicators of inflation expectations have risen recently but are still low; survey-based measures of long-term inflation expectations are largely unchanged.

The economic outlook has improved recently. The Committee anticipates that, with additional gradual adjustments to monetary policy, economic activity will grow at a moderate pace in the medium term, and labor market conditions will stay strong. Inflation over the next year is projected to rise in the coming months and settle around the Committee's 2 percent objective over the medium term. Near-term risks to the economic outlook appear generally balanced, but the Committee is closely observing inflation trends.

The Committee expects that economic conditions will evolve to justify further gradual increases in the policy rate; the policy rate is likely to remain, for a period, below levels anticipated in the longer run. However, the actual trajectory of the policy rate will depend on the economic outlook as informed by incoming information.

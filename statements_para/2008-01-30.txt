The Committee has opted to decrease its primary policy interest rate by half a percentage point.

Financial markets continue to experience significant strain, and access to credit has become more restricted for certain firms and individuals. Furthermore, recent data points to a worsening of the housing downturn and some weakening in employment conditions.

The Committee anticipates that price increases will slow in the upcoming quarters, but close observation of inflation trends will remain essential.

This policy adjustment, alongside previous measures, is intended to foster moderate economic expansion over time and lessen threats to economic activity. Nevertheless, risks to growth remain skewed to the downside. The Committee will persist in evaluating how financial and other factors influence the economic outlook and will respond promptly as necessary to address these risks.

One member, [member], dissented from this decision, preferring to maintain the policy rate at its previous level.

Separately, the Board of Governors unanimously endorsed a half-percentage-point reduction in the discount rate. This action followed approvals of requests from the Boards of Directors of several Federal Reserve Banks.

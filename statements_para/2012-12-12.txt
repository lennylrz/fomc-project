The Committee decided to maintain its current stance on the federal funds rate.

Recent data, since the [month] meeting, indicate that economic activity and job growth have continued at a moderate pace, despite some weather-related disruptions. While the unemployment rate has seen some reduction since summer, it remains elevated. Consumer spending has progressed, and the housing market shows further positive signs, but business investment in fixed assets has slowed. Inflation has been somewhat below the Committee's long-term target, with temporary fluctuations mainly due to energy price changes. Long-term inflation expectations have held steady.

The Committee is concerned that without sufficient policy support, economic growth might not be robust enough to achieve sustained improvements in the job market. Furthermore, ongoing stress in global financial markets presents significant downside risks to the economic outlook. The Committee also anticipates that inflation over the medium term will likely be at or below its 2 percent objective.

To foster a stronger economic recovery and ensure inflation aligns with its dual mandate over time, the Committee will continue its purchases of agency mortgage-backed securities at a pace of $40 billion per month. Following the completion of its program to extend the average maturity of Treasury holdings at year-end, the Committee will begin purchasing longer-term Treasury securities, initially at a pace of $45 billion per month. The Committee will continue its existing policy of reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and in [month], will resume rolling over maturing Treasury securities. These measures are intended to keep downward pressure on long-term interest rates, support mortgage markets, and generally ease financial conditions.

The Committee will closely monitor upcoming economic and financial information. If the job market outlook does not significantly improve, the Committee will continue its purchases of Treasury and agency mortgage-backed securities, and use other policy tools as needed, until such improvement is achieved in a stable price environment. In determining the scale, speed, and composition of asset purchases, the Committee will, as always, assess their likely effectiveness and costs.

To support ongoing progress toward maximum employment and price stability, the Committee expects that a highly accommodative monetary policy will be appropriate for a considerable period after the asset purchase program concludes and the economic recovery strengthens. Specifically, the Committee anticipates that the current exceptionally low range for the federal funds rate will be suitable at least until the unemployment rate falls below 6-1/2 percent, projected inflation one to two years ahead is no more than half a percentage point above the Committee’s 2 percent long-run goal, and long-term inflation expectations remain well-anchored. The Committee views these thresholds as consistent with its previous date-based guidance. In deciding how long to maintain this highly accommodative stance, the Committee will also consider other information, including additional labor market indicators, inflation pressure gauges, inflation expectations, and financial developments. When the Committee decides to begin withdrawing policy accommodation, it will do so in a balanced manner consistent with its long-term goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation.

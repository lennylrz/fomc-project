Today, the Federal Open Market Committee increased its primary policy rate by a quarter of a percentage point.

The Committee assesses that, even following this adjustment, the monetary policy stance continues to be supportive, and when combined with strong underlying productivity gains, it is still bolstering economic activity. In recent periods, the expansion of output has decelerated, and the rate of improvement in employment conditions has lessened. This moderation is likely significantly attributable to the considerable increase in energy costs. Despite this, the economy seems prepared to return to a more vigorous expansionary path in the future. Inflation has been somewhat elevated this year, though a part of this price increase appears to stem from temporary influences.

The Committee judges that the potential for both stronger and weaker outcomes regarding sustained growth and stable prices over the coming quarters are approximately balanced. Given that underlying inflation is still anticipated to be relatively subdued, the Committee believes that the removal of policy support can proceed at a pace that is likely to be gradual. Nevertheless, the Committee will adjust to shifts in economic outlooks as necessary to meet its commitment to preserving price stability.

Separately, the Board of Governors unanimously endorsed a quarter percentage point rise in the discount rate. This action followed approvals of requests from the Boards of Directors of numerous Federal Reserve Banks.

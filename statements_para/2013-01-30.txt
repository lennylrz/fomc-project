The Committee decided to maintain its policy interest rate at its current setting. It will also continue its existing approach of reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities. Furthermore, the Committee will persist with its current pace of acquiring additional agency mortgage-backed securities and longer-term Treasury securities.

Recent data indicates that economic activity experienced a pause in recent months, largely due to temporary factors like adverse weather. Employment has continued to grow moderately, though the jobless rate remains elevated. Household spending and business investment have advanced, and the housing sector has shown further improvement. Inflation has been somewhat below the Committee’s long-term objective, excluding temporary fluctuations mainly from energy prices. Long-term inflation expectations have remained stable.

The Committee anticipates that economic growth will proceed at a moderate pace, and the unemployment rate will gradually decline toward levels consistent with its dual mandate of maximum employment and price stability. Despite some easing in global financial market strains, the Committee still perceives downside risks to the economic outlook. Inflation is expected to be at or below its 2 percent objective over the medium term.

The Committee will closely monitor incoming economic and financial information. If the labor market outlook does not substantially improve, the Committee will continue its asset purchases and use other policy tools as appropriate, until such improvement is achieved alongside price stability. The Committee will consider the efficacy and costs of these purchases when determining their size, pace, and composition.

A highly accommodative monetary policy stance is expected to remain appropriate for a considerable time after the asset purchase program concludes and the economic recovery strengthens. Specifically, the Committee expects its exceptionally low policy rate to be appropriate as long as the unemployment rate stays above 6-1/2 percent, projected inflation one to two years ahead is no more than a half percentage point above the 2 percent long-run goal, and long-term inflation expectations remain well anchored. Other information, including labor market conditions, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial developments, will also be considered. When policy accommodation is eventually removed, a balanced approach consistent with maximum employment and 2 percent inflation will be taken.

One member, [member], dissented from this action, expressing concern that the sustained high level of monetary accommodation increased the risks of future economic and financial imbalances and could, over time, lead to an increase in long-term inflation expectations.

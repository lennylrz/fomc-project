The Committee has opted to decrease its primary interest rate by three-quarters of a percentage point.

Economic activity appears to have deteriorated further, based on recent data. Household spending has decelerated, and employment conditions have softened. Financial markets continue to experience significant strain. Tighter credit availability and a worsening housing downturn are expected to constrain economic expansion in the coming quarters.

Inflation has been high, and some measures of inflation expectations have increased. The Committee anticipates that inflation will ease in the near future, due to an expected stabilization of energy and other commodity costs, and reduced pressure on resource utilization. Nevertheless, the future path of inflation is more uncertain. Close attention to inflation trends will be essential.

This policy adjustment, along with previous steps, including those aimed at improving market liquidity, should support moderate growth over time and lessen threats to economic activity. However, risks to growth remain tilted to the downside. The Committee stands ready to respond promptly as necessary to foster sustained economic growth and price stability.

Recent data, gathered since the [month] meeting, indicate that economic expansion has resumed at a moderate pace following a slowdown late last year. The job market has shown some improvement recently, though the unemployment rate remains elevated. Household spending and business investment have increased, and the housing sector has continued to strengthen. However, government fiscal policy has become somewhat more restrictive. Inflation has been somewhat below the Committee's long-term 2 percent objective, excluding temporary fluctuations mainly due to energy price changes. Long-term inflation expectations have held steady.

The Committee anticipates that, with appropriate policy support, the economy will grow moderately, and the unemployment rate will gradually fall to levels consistent with its dual mandate of maximum employment and price stability. The Committee still perceives risks to the economic outlook as leaning to the downside. It also expects that inflation over the medium term will likely be at or below its 2 percent target.

To bolster a stronger economic recovery and help ensure inflation aligns with its dual mandate over time, the Committee decided to continue its purchases of agency mortgage-backed securities at $40 billion per month and longer-term Treasury securities at $45 billion per month. The Committee will also continue its existing practice of reinvesting principal payments from its agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities. These actions are intended to keep downward pressure on long-term interest rates, support mortgage markets, and make overall financial conditions more accommodative.

The Committee will closely monitor upcoming economic and financial information. It will continue its asset purchases and use other policy tools as needed until the labor market outlook has substantially improved within a context of price stability. In deciding the size, pace, and composition of these purchases, the Committee will consider their likely effectiveness and costs, as well as progress toward its economic goals.

To support ongoing progress toward maximum employment and price stability, the Committee expects that a highly accommodative monetary policy stance will be appropriate for a considerable period after the asset purchase program concludes and the economic recovery strengthens. Specifically, the Committee anticipates that the current exceptionally low range for the policy rate will be appropriate at least as long as the unemployment rate stays above 6-1/2 percent, projected inflation one to two years ahead is no more than a half percentage point above the Committee's 2 percent long-run goal, and long-term inflation expectations remain well anchored. In determining how long to maintain this accommodative stance, the Committee will also consider other information, including additional labor market indicators, inflation pressure and expectation measures, and financial developments. When the Committee decides to begin removing policy accommodation, it will do so in a balanced way consistent with its long-run goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation.

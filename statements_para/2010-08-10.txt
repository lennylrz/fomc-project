The Committee decided to maintain its policy interest rate at its current setting.

Recent data indicate a deceleration in the pace of economic and employment recovery. Consumer spending is gradually increasing, but remains limited by elevated joblessness, modest income growth, reduced housing wealth, and restricted credit access. Business investment in equipment and software is growing, though nonresidential construction remains weak, and firms are hesitant to expand their workforces. Housing starts remain low. Bank lending continues to shrink. Despite these factors, the Committee expects a gradual improvement in resource utilization while maintaining price stability, although the near-term economic recovery is projected to be slower than previously anticipated.

Underlying inflation metrics have shown a downward trend recently. With significant unused capacity limiting cost pressures and stable long-term inflation expectations, inflation is expected to remain low for an extended period.

The Committee anticipates that prevailing economic conditions, including low resource utilization, subdued inflation trends, and stable inflation expectations, will necessitate exceptionally low policy rates for a prolonged duration.

To support economic recovery within a framework of price stability, the Committee will maintain its securities holdings at their current level. This will be achieved by reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and agency mortgage-backed securities into longer-term Treasury securities. The Committee will also continue to roll over maturing Treasury securities.

The Committee will continue to observe economic prospects and financial developments, and will utilize its policy instruments as needed to foster economic recovery and price stability.

[member] dissented from this policy. [member] believes the economy is recovering moderately, as expected. Therefore, [member] felt that continuing to signal exceptionally low policy rates for an extended period was no longer appropriate and restricts the Committee's flexibility to adjust policy when necessary. Furthermore, given current economic and financial conditions, [member] did not believe that maintaining the current size of the Federal Reserve's longer-term securities holdings was essential to achieve the Committee's policy goals.

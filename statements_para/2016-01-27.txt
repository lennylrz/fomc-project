The Committee chose to keep its primary interest rate unchanged at this meeting. It also decided to continue its current approach of reinvesting principal from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into new mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury bonds. This strategy aims to sustain supportive financial conditions by maintaining a substantial portfolio of long-term assets until the federal funds rate has significantly normalized.

Recent data indicate that the job market has shown further improvement, even as overall economic expansion slowed in the latter part of last year. Consumer spending and business investment in fixed assets have seen moderate increases recently, and the housing sector has continued to strengthen. However, foreign trade has been weak, and inventory accumulation has decelerated. Various recent labor market metrics, including robust job creation, suggest a further reduction in underutilized labor resources. Inflation has consistently remained below the Committee's 2 percent long-term target, partly due to falling energy prices and cheaper non-energy imports. Market-based gauges of inflation compensation have decreased further, while survey-based measures of long-term inflation expectations have, on average, shown little change recently.

Looking ahead, the Committee anticipates that, with measured adjustments to monetary policy, economic activity will expand at a moderate pace, and labor market indicators will continue to improve. Inflation is projected to stay low in the near term, partly because of ongoing declines in energy prices, but is expected to reach 2 percent over the medium term as the temporary effects of lower energy and import prices fade and the labor market strengthens further. The Committee is closely observing global economic and financial developments to assess their implications for the labor market, inflation, and the overall balance of risks to the outlook.

In determining future adjustments to the policy rate, the Committee will evaluate actual and projected economic conditions against its goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. This assessment will incorporate a broad range of information, including labor market conditions, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments. Given the current shortfall of inflation from 2 percent, the Committee will carefully monitor progress toward its inflation objective. The Committee expects that economic conditions will evolve in a way that warrants only gradual increases in the federal funds rate, which is likely to remain below its longer-run equilibrium for some time. However, the actual path of the federal funds rate will depend on the economic outlook as informed by incoming data.

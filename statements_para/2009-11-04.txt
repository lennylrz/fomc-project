The Committee decided to keep its policy interest rate unchanged.

Recent data indicate continued expansion in economic activity since the [month] meeting. Financial market conditions have shown little overall change. The housing sector has seen increased activity recently. Consumer spending appears to be growing, though it faces headwinds from job losses, slow income growth, reduced housing wealth, and restricted credit access. Businesses are still reducing investment and employment, but at a slower pace, and are better aligning inventories with sales.

While economic activity is expected to remain weak for a period, the Committee foresees that measures to stabilize financial markets and institutions, along with fiscal and monetary stimulus and market dynamics, will foster stronger economic growth and a gradual increase in resource utilization, consistent with stable prices.

Given significant unused capacity likely to keep cost pressures low and stable long-term inflation expectations, the Committee anticipates that inflation will stay low for some time.

Under these circumstances, the Federal Reserve will continue to use various instruments to support economic recovery and maintain price stability. The Committee will maintain its policy rate at its current level and expects that economic conditions, including underutilized resources, low inflation trends, and stable inflation expectations, will likely necessitate exceptionally low policy rates for an extended duration. To support mortgage lending, housing markets, and overall private credit conditions, the Federal Reserve will acquire a total of $1.25 trillion in agency mortgage-backed securities and approximately $175 billion in agency debt. The agency debt purchase amount, though below the previously stated maximum, aligns with recent purchase patterns and reflects limited availability. To ensure a smooth market transition, the Committee will gradually reduce the pace of its purchases of both agency debt and agency mortgage-backed securities, with these transactions expected to conclude by the end of the first quarter of [year+1]. The Committee will continue to assess the timing and total volume of its security purchases based on the evolving economic outlook and financial market conditions. The Federal Reserve is monitoring its balance sheet's size and composition and will adjust its credit and liquidity programs as needed.

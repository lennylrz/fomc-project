The Committee decided to keep its policy interest rate unchanged.

Recent data indicate ongoing strengthening in economic activity, with initial signs of improvement in the employment situation. Consumer spending has recently accelerated but faces headwinds from elevated joblessness, limited income growth, reduced housing equity, and restricted credit access. Business investment in equipment and software has seen considerable gains, though nonresidential construction is contracting, and firms remain hesitant to expand their workforces. Housing starts have slightly increased but stay at very low levels. Despite continued contraction in bank lending, financial markets generally support economic expansion. The Committee foresees a moderate pace of economic recovery for a period, followed by a gradual increase in resource utilization while maintaining stable prices.

Given significant underutilization of resources, which is dampening cost pressures, and steady long-term inflation expectations, price increases are expected to remain low for an extended duration.

The Committee expects that prevailing economic conditions, including low resource utilization, muted inflation trends, and stable inflation expectations, will necessitate maintaining exceptionally low levels of its policy rate for an extended period. The Committee will continue to observe economic projections and financial developments, using its policy instruments as needed to foster economic recovery and price stability.

Due to better functioning financial markets, the Federal Reserve has concluded most of its special liquidity programs established during the crisis. The Term Asset-Backed Securities Loan Facility, the sole remaining program, is set to conclude on [month] 30 for new commercial mortgage-backed securities loans, having ended on [month] 31 for all other collateral types.

One member, [member], dissented from this policy action. This member believed that continuing to signal exceptionally low policy rates for an extended period was no longer appropriate. This stance, in their view, could foster future imbalances, heighten risks to long-term macroeconomic and financial stability, and constrain the Committee's ability to initiate modest rate increases.

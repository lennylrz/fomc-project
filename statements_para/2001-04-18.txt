Today, the Committee opted to decrease its primary interest rate by half a percentage point. Concurrently, the Board of Governors endorsed a similar half-percentage-point reduction in the discount rate.

Regarding the economic landscape since the [month] gathering, the Committee observed that the process of reducing surplus stockpiles appears to be largely complete. Household spending and residential investment have shown resilience, though their recent momentum has leveled off. While productivity measures likely softened in the initial quarter, the underlying trend of strong growth established in prior years seems to persist.

However, business investment continues to decline. The ongoing erosion of current and anticipated profits, coupled with increased uncertainty regarding future business conditions, is expected to further restrain capital expenditures. This potential drag, alongside the possible impact of past declines in stock market wealth on consumer spending and the risk of slower international growth, poses a threat of unacceptably sluggish economic activity. Consequently, the Committee concluded that a policy adjustment is appropriate during this extended period between meetings.

The Committee maintains its view that, considering its long-term objectives of stable prices and sustained economic expansion, and based on current information, the predominant risks lean towards conditions that could lead to economic weakness in the near future.

The Federal Reserve Board's approval of the discount rate change followed requests from the Boards of Directors of the Federal Reserve Banks of Boston, New York, Philadelphia, Cleveland, Atlanta, Minneapolis, Dallas, and San Francisco.

Today, the Committee increased its primary policy rate by a quarter of a percentage point. Concurrently, the Board of Governors unanimously endorsed a quarter-percentage-point rise in the discount rate, following requests from numerous Federal Reserve Banks.

Economic activity shows signs of slowing down from its earlier robust expansion this year. This deceleration is partly attributable to a cooling housing sector and the delayed impact of prior rate hikes and energy cost increases.

Regarding price stability, core inflation metrics have been elevated recently. While sustained productivity improvements have helped contain unit labor cost growth and inflation expectations are stable, significant resource utilization and high energy and commodity prices could maintain upward pressure on prices.

Looking ahead, while a slowdown in overall demand should eventually help curb inflation, the Committee perceives lingering inflation-related risks. The necessity, magnitude, and timing of any further policy tightening to address these risks will be determined by how the inflation and growth outlooks evolve, based on new data. The Committee stands ready to adjust its approach as economic conditions change to meet its objectives.

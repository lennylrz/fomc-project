The Federal Open Market Committee has unanimously endorsed revisions to its foundational document outlining its long-term objectives and monetary policy approach. This updated statement reflects economic shifts over the past decade and how policymakers are integrating these changes into their decision-making. The aim is to boost the clarity, accountability, and effectiveness of monetary policy.

A strong job market is beneficial, especially for those in lower and middle-income communities, and can be sustained without causing an undesirable rise in inflation.

Regarding maximum employment, the Committee now emphasizes that its policy choices will be guided by its assessment of employment shortfalls from its highest sustainable level, rather than deviations.

For price stability, the Committee has adjusted its strategy to pursue an average inflation rate of 2 percent over time. Consequently, after periods where inflation has consistently fallen short of 2 percent, appropriate policy will likely aim for inflation to moderately exceed 2 percent for some duration.

These updates explicitly acknowledge the difficulties for monetary policy presented by a persistently low interest rate environment, where policy rates are more frequently constrained by their effective lower bound than in the past.

The Committee will continue its annual review of this statement each [month] and plans a public re-evaluation of its strategy, tools, and communication practices approximately every five years.

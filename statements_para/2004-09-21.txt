The Committee has opted to increase its primary policy interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point.

This adjustment notwithstanding, the Committee judges that the current monetary policy stance continues to be supportive, and when combined with strong foundational productivity gains, it is fostering economic expansion. Following a slowdown earlier this year, partly due to a significant increase in energy costs, economic output seems to have picked up, and conditions in the employment sector have shown slight improvement. Despite the surge in energy prices, both the rate of price increases and future price expectations have moderated recently.

The Committee assesses that the potential for both stronger and weaker outcomes regarding sustained growth and stable prices over the coming quarters are approximately balanced. Given that underlying price increases are anticipated to remain relatively subdued, the Committee believes that the withdrawal of policy support can proceed at a gradual pace. However, the Committee stands ready to adjust its approach in response to evolving economic conditions to meet its commitment to maintaining price stability.

Separately, the Board of Governors unanimously endorsed a quarter percentage point increase in the discount rate. This decision followed approvals from the Boards of Directors of the Federal Reserve Banks of Boston, New York, Philadelphia, Cleveland, Richmond, Atlanta, Chicago, St. Louis, Minneapolis, Kansas City, Dallas, and San Francisco.

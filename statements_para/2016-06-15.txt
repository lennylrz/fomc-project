The Committee chose to keep its policy interest rate unchanged at this meeting. This decision means that the current monetary policy stance continues to support job growth and the achievement of the 2 percent inflation target.

Recent data, gathered since the [month] meeting, indicate a pickup in overall economic activity, though the pace of job market improvement has slowed. While the unemployment rate has fallen, the number of new jobs created has decreased. Consumer spending has increased. The housing sector has shown continued improvement this year, and the negative impact from international trade appears to have lessened. However, business investment in fixed assets has been weak. Inflation remains below the Committee's 2 percent long-term goal, partly due to earlier drops in energy and imported goods prices. Market-based measures of future inflation expectations have declined, but most survey-based measures of long-term inflation expectations have been largely stable recently.

The Committee's mandate is to promote maximum employment and stable prices. It anticipates that, with gradual adjustments to monetary policy, the economy will grow moderately, and the job market will strengthen. Inflation is expected to stay low in the near term, partly because of past energy price declines, but should rise to 2 percent over the medium term as the temporary effects of lower energy and import prices fade and the labor market improves further. The Committee will continue to closely watch inflation indicators and global economic and financial developments.

When considering future adjustments to the policy rate, the Committee will evaluate current and projected economic conditions against its goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. This assessment will incorporate various data, including labor market indicators, measures of inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments. Given that inflation is currently below 2 percent, the Committee will carefully monitor progress toward its inflation objective. The Committee expects that economic conditions will evolve in a way that warrants only gradual increases in the policy rate, which is likely to remain below its long-run equilibrium for some time. However, the actual path of the policy rate will depend on the economic outlook as new data become available.

The Committee will continue its current practice of reinvesting principal payments from its agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities. This practice is expected to continue until the normalization of the policy rate is well advanced. By maintaining a substantial level of longer-term securities, this approach helps ensure accommodative financial conditions.

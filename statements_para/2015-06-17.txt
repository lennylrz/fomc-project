The Committee decided to maintain its policy interest rate at its current level.

Recent data indicate a moderate expansion of economic activity following a period of little change in the first quarter. Job growth accelerated, and the unemployment rate held steady. Overall, various labor market metrics suggest a slight reduction in underemployment. Household spending increased moderately, and the housing sector showed some improvement. However, business fixed investment and net exports remained weak. Inflation stayed below the Committee's long-term goal, partly due to previous drops in energy prices and declining non-energy import costs; energy prices now appear stable. Market-based inflation compensation measures are still low, while survey-based long-term inflation expectations have been stable.

The Committee expects economic activity to grow at a moderate pace with appropriate policy support, and labor market indicators to continue moving towards levels consistent with its dual mandate of maximum employment and price stability. The risks to the economic and labor market outlook are still considered nearly balanced. Inflation is projected to stay near its recent low level in the short term but is expected to gradually approach 2 percent over the medium term as the labor market strengthens further and the temporary effects of past energy and import price declines fade. The Committee continues to closely monitor inflation trends.

To support ongoing progress toward its objectives, the Committee reaffirmed that the current policy rate range remains appropriate. In deciding how long to keep this range, the Committee will evaluate progress, both observed and anticipated, toward maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. This assessment will incorporate a broad array of information, including labor market conditions, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments. The Committee anticipates that an increase in the policy rate range will be suitable once further labor market improvement is evident and there is reasonable confidence that inflation will return to its 2 percent objective over the medium term.

The Committee will continue its current practice of reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities. This approach helps maintain accommodative financial conditions by keeping substantial holdings of longer-term securities.

When the Committee decides to begin withdrawing policy accommodation, it will adopt a balanced approach consistent with its long-run goals. The Committee currently expects that, even when employment and inflation are near mandate-consistent levels, economic conditions may, for some time, justify keeping the policy rate below levels considered normal in the long run.

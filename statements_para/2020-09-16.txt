The Committee chose to maintain its policy interest rate at its current level.

Recent economic data indicate an improvement in activity and job growth, though both remain significantly below pre-[event] levels. Consumer price increases are subdued due to reduced demand and lower energy costs. Financial conditions have generally improved, partly due to policy support.

The economic trajectory is highly dependent on the virus's progression. The ongoing health crisis will continue to suppress economic activity, employment, and inflation in the near term, and presents substantial risks to the medium-term outlook.

The Committee aims for maximum employment and a 2 percent inflation rate over the long run. Given persistent undershooting of this inflation target, the Committee intends to allow inflation to moderately surpass 2 percent for a period, ensuring a 2 percent average over time and stable long-term inflation expectations. An accommodative monetary policy stance will be maintained until these objectives are met. The Committee anticipates keeping the policy rate unchanged until labor market conditions align with maximum employment assessments and inflation reaches 2 percent, with a trajectory to moderately exceed it for some time. Furthermore, in the coming months, the Federal Reserve will continue to expand its holdings of Treasury and mortgage-backed securities at least at the current rate to ensure market stability and foster supportive financial conditions, thereby facilitating credit flow to households and businesses.

In evaluating the appropriate policy stance, the Committee will continue to assess incoming information's implications for the economic outlook. Should risks arise that could hinder the achievement of its goals, the Committee stands ready to adjust policy as needed. These assessments will incorporate various data, including public health metrics, labor market indicators, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments.

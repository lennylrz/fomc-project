Since the last meeting in [month], the job market has continued to improve, and overall economic activity has seen continued moderate expansion. Employment gains have been robust, and the unemployment rate has shown little change recently. Household spending has maintained a moderate upward trend, and business fixed investment appears to have gained some strength. Inflation has risen in recent quarters, nearing the Committee's 2 percent long-term target; however, when excluding volatile food and energy prices, inflation has remained largely stable and somewhat below 2 percent. Market-based indicators of inflation compensation are still low, while survey-based measures of longer-term inflation expectations have, on average, shown little movement.

The Committee's mandate is to promote maximum employment and price stability. It anticipates that, with gradual adjustments to monetary policy, economic activity will expand moderately, labor market conditions will strengthen further, and inflation will stabilize around 2 percent over the medium term. The immediate risks to the economic outlook appear to be generally balanced. The Committee continues to closely observe inflation metrics and global economic and financial developments.

Given current and projected labor market conditions and inflation, the Committee chose to raise the target range for the federal funds rate. Monetary policy remains supportive, which should further strengthen the labor market and help inflation return sustainably to 2 percent.

When considering future adjustments to the policy rate, the Committee will evaluate economic conditions against its goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. This assessment will incorporate various data, including labor market indicators, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments. The Committee will carefully monitor actual and expected inflation relative to its symmetric inflation objective. The Committee expects that economic conditions will evolve to justify gradual increases in the federal funds rate, which is likely to remain below its long-run expected level for some time. However, the actual path of the federal funds rate will depend on incoming economic data.

The Committee will continue its current practice of reinvesting principal payments from its agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities. This is expected to continue until the normalization of the federal funds rate is well advanced. This approach, by maintaining substantial holdings of longer-term securities, should help keep financial conditions accommodative.

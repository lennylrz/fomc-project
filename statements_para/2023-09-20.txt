The Committee chose to keep its primary interest rate unchanged at this meeting.

Economic activity has shown robust expansion recently. While job growth has moderated in recent months, it continues to be strong, and the unemployment rate is still low. Inflationary pressures persist at an elevated level. The U.S. banking sector is robust and stable. It is probable that stricter lending conditions for consumers and businesses will dampen economic activity, employment, and price increases. The full impact of these conditions is not yet clear. The Committee remains very focused on the dangers posed by inflation.

The Committee aims for maximum employment and a 2 percent inflation rate over the long term. To support these objectives, the Committee will continue to reduce its holdings of government bonds and agency mortgage-backed securities, as previously outlined. The Committee is firmly dedicated to bringing inflation back to its 2 percent target.

Future policy adjustments will depend on new information and its implications for the economy. When considering if further policy tightening is needed to achieve the 2 percent inflation target over time, the Committee will consider the total amount of policy tightening already implemented, the delayed effects of monetary policy on the economy and inflation, and overall economic and financial trends. The Committee will also be ready to modify its policy stance if new risks arise that could hinder the achievement of its goals. These evaluations will incorporate a broad range of data, including labor market indicators, inflation pressures and expectations, and global financial developments.

The Committee decided to keep its policy interest rate unchanged. It will continue its current pace of purchasing agency mortgage-backed securities and longer-term Treasury securities. The existing approach of reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities, will also be maintained. These actions are intended to sustain downward pressure on long-term interest rates, support mortgage markets, and foster more accommodating financial conditions.

Recent data indicates that economic activity has been expanding at a moderate rate. The labor market has shown some improvement recently, but the unemployment rate remains elevated. Household spending and business fixed investment have advanced, and the housing sector has strengthened further. However, fiscal policy is currently constraining economic growth. Inflation has been running somewhat below the Committee's long-term objective, excluding temporary variations primarily due to energy price fluctuations. Long-term inflation expectations have remained stable.

The Committee anticipates that, with appropriate policy support, economic growth will continue at a moderate pace, and the unemployment rate will gradually decrease towards levels consistent with its dual mandate of maximum employment and price stability. The Committee still perceives downside risks to the economic outlook. It also expects that inflation over the medium term will likely be at or below its 2 percent objective.

The Committee will closely monitor incoming economic and financial information. It will continue its asset purchases and use other policy tools as needed until the labor market outlook has substantially improved within a context of price stability. The Committee is prepared to adjust the pace of its purchases to maintain appropriate policy accommodation if the outlook for the labor market or inflation changes. In determining the size, pace, and composition of asset purchases, the Committee will consider their likely effectiveness and costs, as well as progress toward its economic objectives.

A highly accommodative monetary policy stance is expected to remain appropriate for a considerable time after the asset purchase program concludes and the economic recovery strengthens. Specifically, the Committee anticipates that this exceptionally low policy rate will be appropriate at least as long as the unemployment rate stays above 6-1/2 percent, inflation projected one to two years ahead is no more than a half percentage point above the Committee's 2 percent long-run goal, and long-term inflation expectations remain well anchored. In deciding how long to maintain this accommodative stance, the Committee will also consider other information, including additional labor market measures, indicators of inflation pressures and expectations, and financial developments. When the Committee decides to begin removing policy accommodation, it will take a balanced approach consistent with its long-run goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation.

The Committee chose to maintain its policy interest rate at its current setting. It also decided to continue its existing asset purchase program, acquiring agency mortgage-backed securities at a pace of $40 billion each month and longer-term Treasury securities at a pace of $45 billion each month. The current practice of reinvesting principal payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities, will persist. These actions are intended to keep downward pressure on long-term interest rates, support mortgage markets, and foster more accommodating financial conditions.

Recent data, since the [month] meeting, indicate that economic activity has been expanding at a moderate rate. The job market has shown further improvement recently, though the unemployment rate remains elevated. Household spending and business investment have increased, and the housing sector has strengthened. However, government fiscal policy is constraining economic growth. Inflation has been below the Committee's long-term objective, partly due to temporary factors, but long-term inflation expectations have remained stable.

The Committee anticipates that, with appropriate policy support, the economy will grow moderately, and the unemployment rate will gradually fall towards levels consistent with its dual mandate of maximum employment and price stability. The risks to the economic and labor market outlook have lessened since the fall. The Committee also expects that inflation will likely be at or below its 2 percent objective over the medium term.

The Committee will continue its purchases of Treasury and agency mortgage-backed securities, and use other policy tools as needed, until the labor market outlook has substantially improved within a context of price stability. The Committee is prepared to adjust the pace of its purchases to maintain appropriate policy accommodation if the outlook for the labor market or inflation changes. In determining the size, pace, and composition of asset purchases, the Committee will consider their likely effectiveness and costs, as well as progress toward its economic objectives.

A highly accommodative monetary policy stance will likely be appropriate for a considerable period after the asset purchase program concludes and the economic recovery strengthens. Specifically, the Committee expects that the current low range for the federal funds rate will be appropriate as long as the unemployment rate stays above 6-1/2 percent, projected inflation one to two years ahead is no more than a half percentage point above the 2 percent long-run goal, and long-term inflation expectations remain well anchored. Other factors, such as additional labor market measures, inflation pressure indicators, inflation expectations, and financial developments, will also be considered when determining how long to maintain this accommodative stance. When policy accommodation is eventually removed, a balanced approach consistent with long-run goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation will be adopted.

Two members dissented from this action. [member] believed the Committee should more strongly signal its commitment to its inflation goal given recent low inflation readings. [member] was concerned that the continued high level of monetary accommodation increased the risks of future economic and financial imbalances and could, over time, lead to an increase in long-term inflation expectations.

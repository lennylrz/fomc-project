The Committee decided to keep its policy interest rate unchanged.

Recent data indicate that the economy is continuing its recovery, and the employment situation is slowly getting better. Consumer spending is up, but it's still held back by many people being out of work, slow income growth, reduced home equity, and difficulty getting loans. Businesses are spending much more on machinery and software, but construction of commercial buildings is still weak, and companies are hesitant to hire more staff. New home construction remains very low. Overall, financial conditions have become less favorable for economic expansion, mainly due to international events. Bank lending has continued to shrink in recent months. Despite these factors, the Committee expects that the economy will gradually return to fuller capacity while maintaining stable prices, though the speed of the recovery will probably be modest for a while.

Energy and other raw material prices have fallen somewhat recently, and core inflation has been trending downwards. With significant unused capacity still limiting cost increases and long-term inflation expectations stable, inflation is expected to remain low for some time.

The Committee will continue to observe economic projections and financial changes, and will use its policy instruments as needed to support economic recovery and price stability.

One member, [member], voted against this policy. This member felt that stating an expectation of exceptionally low policy rates for an extended period was no longer appropriate. This was because it could contribute to future problems and increase dangers to long-term economic and financial stability, while also reducing the Committee's ability to start raising rates gradually.

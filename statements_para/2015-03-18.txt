The Committee decided to maintain its policy interest rate at its current setting. It will also continue its existing approach to reinvesting principal payments from its agency debt and mortgage-backed securities holdings into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities. This strategy aims to preserve supportive financial conditions.

Recent data indicates a slight slowdown in economic expansion since [month]. The job market has shown further improvement, with robust job creation and a reduced unemployment rate. Various labor market metrics suggest that underutilization of labor resources continues to lessen. Household spending is increasing moderately, aided by lower energy prices boosting purchasing power. Business fixed investment is progressing, though the housing sector's recovery remains sluggish, and export growth has weakened. Inflation has fallen further below the Committee's long-term target, primarily due to declining energy costs. Market-based inflation compensation measures are still low, while survey-based long-term inflation expectations have held steady.

The Committee expects moderate economic growth and continued labor market improvement toward its dual mandate objectives, assuming appropriate policy accommodation. Risks to the economic and labor market outlook are considered nearly balanced. Inflation is projected to stay near its current low level in the short term but is anticipated to gradually rise toward 2 percent over the medium term as the labor market strengthens and temporary effects from energy price declines and other factors fade. The Committee is closely monitoring inflation trends.

In determining how long to keep the policy rate unchanged, the Committee will evaluate progress, both actual and anticipated, toward maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. This assessment will incorporate a broad range of information, including labor market conditions, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments. An increase in the policy rate remains improbable at the [month] meeting. The Committee expects to raise the policy rate when it observes further labor market improvement and is reasonably confident that inflation will return to its 2 percent objective over the medium term. This updated guidance does not imply a decision on the timing of the initial rate hike.

When the Committee eventually begins to withdraw policy accommodation, it will adopt a balanced approach consistent with its long-term goals. Even after employment and inflation approach mandate-consistent levels, economic conditions may, for some time, necessitate keeping the policy rate below levels considered normal in the long run.

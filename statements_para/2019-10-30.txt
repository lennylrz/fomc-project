The Committee chose to decrease its policy interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point at this gathering.

While consumer spending has shown considerable strength, business investment in fixed assets and exports continue to be weak. Over the past year, both headline inflation and core inflation (excluding food and energy) are below the 2 percent target. Market-derived indicators of inflation compensation are still low, and survey-based measures of long-term inflation expectations have shown little change.

This decision reflects the Committee's expectation that sustained economic growth, a healthy job market, and inflation around its symmetrical 2 percent goal are the most probable outcomes. However, the future remains uncertain. The Committee will continue to observe incoming data to determine the appropriate course for the policy rate.

Future adjustments to the policy rate will depend on an evaluation of current and projected economic conditions against the Committee's goals of maximum employment and symmetrical 2 percent inflation. This evaluation will consider various factors, including labor market metrics, inflation pressures and expectations, and global financial developments.

The Committee decided to keep its policy interest rate unchanged.

Recent data indicate ongoing economic expansion, with some improvement in the employment situation. Consumer spending is growing moderately, though it faces headwinds from a weak job market, limited income growth, reduced housing wealth, and tight credit. Business investment in equipment and software shows signs of increasing, but construction spending continues to decline, and companies remain hesitant to expand their workforces. Inventory levels are now better aligned with sales. While bank lending remains constrained, financial conditions generally support economic growth.

The Committee expects a gradual return to full resource utilization and price stability, though the pace of recovery is likely to be moderate for a period. With significant unused capacity limiting cost pressures and stable long-term inflation expectations, inflation is projected to remain low for some time.

The Committee anticipates that current economic conditions, including underutilized resources, low inflation trends, and stable inflation expectations, will necessitate maintaining exceptionally low policy rates for an extended duration. To support mortgage markets and housing, and to improve overall private credit conditions, the Federal Reserve is acquiring agency mortgage-backed securities and agency debt. The pace of these acquisitions is being gradually reduced to ensure a smooth market transition, with completion expected by the end of the first quarter. The Committee will continue to assess its asset purchases based on the evolving economic outlook and financial market conditions.

Given improvements in financial market functioning, several temporary liquidity facilities will be closed on [month] 1, as previously announced. These include facilities for asset-backed commercial paper, commercial paper funding, primary dealers, and term securities lending. Additionally, temporary currency swap arrangements with other central banks will conclude on [month] 1. The Term Auction Facility is being phased out, with $50 billion in 28-day credit offered on [month] 8 and a final $25 billion auction on [month] 8. The Term Asset-Backed Securities Loan Facility is still set to expire on [month] 30 for new-issue commercial mortgage-backed securities and [month] 31 for other collateral types. The Federal Reserve stands ready to adjust these plans if needed to support financial stability and economic growth.

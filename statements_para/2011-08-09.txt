The Committee has chosen to maintain its primary policy interest rate at its current level.

Recent data indicates that economic expansion this year has been considerably less robust than previously projected. The employment situation has worsened in recent months, with an increase in joblessness. Household spending has stagnated, investment in commercial structures remains weak, and the housing market is still struggling. However, business investment in equipment and software continues to grow. Only a portion of this recent economic weakness appears attributable to temporary factors, such as the impact of higher food and energy costs on consumer purchasing power and supply chain disruptions stemming from events in [country]. Inflation rose earlier in the year, primarily due to increased prices for certain commodities and imported goods, alongside supply chain issues. More recently, inflation has eased as energy and some commodity prices have fallen from their peaks. Long-term inflation expectations have remained stable.

The Committee now expects a slower pace of recovery in the coming quarters than previously anticipated, with the unemployment rate projected to decline only gradually towards levels consistent with its dual mandate of maximum employment and price stability. Furthermore, risks to the economic outlook have increased on the downside. The Committee also expects inflation to settle at or below mandate-consistent levels in the coming quarters as the impact of past energy and other commodity price increases diminishes. Nevertheless, the Committee will closely monitor the evolution of inflation and inflation expectations.

To support the ongoing economic recovery and ensure long-term inflation consistent with its mandate, the Committee decided to keep its policy rate unchanged. The Committee currently expects that economic conditions, including underutilized resources and a subdued medium-term inflation outlook, will likely necessitate exceptionally low policy rates at least through mid-[year+2]. The Committee will also continue its practice of reinvesting principal payments from its securities. The size and composition of its securities holdings will be regularly reviewed, and adjustments will be made as needed.

The Committee considered the various policy instruments available to foster a stronger economic recovery while maintaining price stability. It will continue to evaluate the economic outlook based on incoming information and stands ready to utilize these instruments as appropriate.

The Committee has chosen to maintain its primary policy interest rate at its current level. It also decided to continue its current pace of asset acquisitions, purchasing $40 billion in mortgage-backed securities and $45 billion in Treasury bonds each month. The existing practice of reinvesting principal from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into new mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury bonds, will persist.

Recent data indicates a moderate expansion of economic activity. The job market has shown further improvement, though the unemployment rate remains elevated. Household spending and business investment have advanced, and the housing sector is strengthening, despite rising mortgage rates and restrictive fiscal policy. Inflation, excluding energy price volatility, is below the 2 percent long-term target, but long-term inflation expectations are stable.

The Committee anticipates that with appropriate policy support, economic growth will accelerate, and the unemployment rate will gradually fall to levels consistent with its dual mandate of maximum employment and price stability. Downside risks to the economy and labor market have lessened since last fall. However, sustained tightening of financial conditions could impede economic and labor market progress. Persistent inflation below 2 percent could pose risks, but the Committee expects it to return to target over the medium term.

The Committee believes that the economic and labor market improvements since the asset purchase program began a year ago reflect growing underlying strength, even with federal fiscal tightening. However, it will wait for more sustained evidence of progress before altering the pace of purchases. These actions aim to keep long-term interest rates low, support mortgage markets, and ease financial conditions, thereby fostering economic recovery and ensuring inflation aligns with the dual mandate over time.

The Committee will closely monitor economic and financial developments. Asset purchases and other policy tools will continue until the labor market outlook substantially improves within a stable price environment. Future decisions on the pace of asset purchases will depend on incoming information supporting ongoing labor market improvement and inflation returning to target. Asset purchases are not predetermined and will be contingent on the economic outlook and an assessment of their efficacy and costs.

A highly accommodative monetary policy will remain appropriate for a considerable period after asset purchases conclude and the recovery strengthens. The exceptionally low range for the federal funds rate is expected to be suitable as long as the unemployment rate stays above 6-1/2 percent, projected inflation one to two years ahead is no more than half a percentage point above the 2 percent goal, and long-term inflation expectations remain anchored. Other factors, including labor market measures, inflation pressures, expectations, and financial developments, will also be considered. When policy accommodation is eventually removed, a balanced approach consistent with maximum employment and 2 percent inflation will be adopted.

One member, [member], dissented, expressing concern that the continued high level of monetary accommodation increased the risks of future economic and financial imbalances and could, over time, lead to an increase in long-term inflation expectations.

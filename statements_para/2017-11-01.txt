The Committee chose to keep its policy interest rate unchanged at this meeting.

Recent data indicate continued strengthening in the job market and solid economic expansion, despite temporary disruptions from recent hurricanes. While these storms caused a dip in payrolls in [month], the unemployment rate nevertheless fell further. Household spending has grown moderately, and business investment has increased in recent quarters. Gasoline prices rose after the hurricanes, boosting overall inflation in [month], but core inflation remained subdued. Over the past year, both overall and core inflation measures have decreased and are below 2 percent. Market-based inflation expectations remain low, while survey-based long-term expectations are largely stable.

The Committee aims to achieve maximum employment and stable prices, as mandated. Hurricane impacts on economic activity, employment, and inflation are expected to be temporary, with no significant long-term effect on the national economy. Therefore, the Committee anticipates moderate economic growth and further job market improvement with gradual policy adjustments. Inflation is expected to stay somewhat below 2 percent in the near term, then stabilize around the 2 percent target over the medium term. Near-term economic risks appear balanced, but the Committee is closely watching inflation trends.

Given current and projected labor market and inflation conditions, the Committee decided to maintain its current policy rate. This accommodative stance supports further job market gains and a sustained return to 2 percent inflation.

Future adjustments to the policy rate will depend on how economic conditions, both realized and expected, align with the Committee's goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. This assessment will consider various data, including labor market indicators, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments. The Committee will closely monitor inflation relative to its symmetric 2 percent objective. The Committee expects gradual increases in the policy rate over time, which will likely remain below long-run equilibrium levels for some period. However, the actual path of the policy rate will be guided by incoming economic data.

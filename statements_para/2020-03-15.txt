The Committee decided to reduce its policy interest rate by one percentage point.

The [event] outbreak has significantly impacted communities and economic activity globally, including in the United States. Global financial conditions have also been notably affected. Prior to this challenging period, the U.S. economy was robust. Data since the [month] meeting indicate a strong labor market through [month] and moderate economic growth. Job creation has been consistent recently, and unemployment remains low. While household spending grew moderately, business fixed investment and exports were weak. The energy sector has recently faced difficulties. Over the past year, both overall inflation and core inflation are below 2 percent. Market-based inflation expectations have fallen, but survey-based longer-term expectations are stable.

The Committee aims for maximum employment and price stability. The [event] will likely constrain economic activity in the near term and presents risks to the economic outlook. This rate reduction is intended to support economic activity, a strong labor market, and inflation returning to the Committee's symmetric 2 percent objective.

The Committee anticipates maintaining this policy rate until confidence is established that the economy has recovered from recent events and is progressing toward its maximum employment and price stability goals. The Committee will continue to assess incoming information, including public health data, global developments, and subdued inflation, and will act as needed to support the economy. Future policy adjustments will be based on realized and expected economic conditions relative to its maximum employment and symmetric 2 percent inflation objectives. This assessment will consider labor market indicators, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments.

The Federal Reserve is prepared to utilize all available tools to ensure credit flow to households and businesses, thereby promoting its dual mandate. To facilitate smooth functioning in Treasury and agency mortgage-backed securities markets, crucial for credit flow, the Committee will increase its holdings of Treasury securities by at least $500 billion and agency mortgage-backed securities by at least $200 billion in the coming months. All principal payments from agency debt and agency mortgage-backed securities will be reinvested into agency mortgage-backed securities. Additionally, the Open Market Desk has expanded its overnight and term repurchase agreement operations. The Committee will closely monitor market conditions and adjust plans as necessary.

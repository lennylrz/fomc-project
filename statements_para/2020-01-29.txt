The Committee chose to keep its policy interest rate unchanged. This decision aligns with the view that the current monetary policy setting is suitable for sustaining economic growth, robust employment, and inflation moving back towards the Committee's 2 percent target.

Recent data indicates a strong labor market, with consistent job creation over the past few months and a low unemployment rate. Economic activity has been expanding at a moderate pace. While consumer spending has shown moderate growth, business investment and exports continue to be weak. Inflation, both overall and excluding volatile items, remains below 2 percent over the last year. Measures of inflation compensation derived from financial markets are still low, and long-term inflation expectations from surveys have shown little change.

Looking ahead, the Committee will closely observe new economic information, including global trends and persistent low inflation, to determine the future course of its policy rate. Future adjustments to the policy rate will depend on an evaluation of actual and anticipated economic conditions against the Committee's goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. This evaluation will consider various factors, such as labor market indicators, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments.

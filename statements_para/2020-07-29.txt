The Committee decided to keep its policy interest rate unchanged.

Economic activity and employment have shown some recovery recently, though they remain substantially below levels seen earlier this year. Consumer price inflation is being suppressed by reduced demand and significantly lower oil prices. Financial conditions have generally improved, partly due to policy actions aimed at supporting the economy and credit availability.

The trajectory of the economy is highly dependent on the progression of the virus. The current public health emergency will significantly impact economic activity, employment, and inflation in the short term, and presents considerable dangers to the economic outlook over the medium term.

The Committee anticipates maintaining the current policy rate until it is assured that the economy has overcome recent challenges and is progressing toward its objectives of maximum employment and price stability. To support credit flow, the Federal Reserve will continue to increase its holdings of Treasury and mortgage-backed securities at least at the current rate in the coming months to ensure smooth market operations. Additionally, large-scale overnight and term repurchase agreement operations will persist. The Committee will carefully observe developments and stands ready to modify its plans as needed.

Future adjustments to monetary policy will be based on an evaluation of actual and anticipated economic conditions against the maximum employment objective and the symmetric 2 percent inflation target. This evaluation will consider various data, including labor market indicators, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments. The Committee will continue to track incoming information, including public health data, global events, and subdued inflation, and will act as necessary to support the economy.

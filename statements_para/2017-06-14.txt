The Committee chose to increase its policy interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point. This action reflects the Committee's view that current and anticipated conditions in the job market and regarding price stability justify the adjustment. The current monetary policy stance remains supportive, fostering further improvements in employment and a sustained return to the 2 percent inflation target.

Economic data received since the [month] meeting indicates continued strengthening in the labor market and moderate growth in overall economic activity this year. Job creation has been consistent, on average, since the start of the year, though at a more moderate pace, and the jobless rate has fallen. Consumer spending has increased recently, and business investment in fixed assets has continued to grow. Over the past year, inflation has recently decreased and, similar to the core measure, is somewhat below 2 percent. Market-based indicators of inflation expectations are still low, while survey-based measures of longer-term inflation expectations show little overall change.

The Committee anticipates that, with measured adjustments to monetary policy, economic expansion will continue at a moderate pace, and labor market conditions will improve further. Inflation, measured over 12 months, is expected to stay somewhat below 2 percent in the near term but to reach the Committee's 2 percent objective over the medium term. The immediate risks to the economic outlook appear generally balanced, but the Committee is closely observing inflation trends.

Regarding future policy adjustments, the Committee will assess economic conditions against its goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation. This evaluation will consider various data, including labor market indicators, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments. The Committee will carefully monitor actual and expected inflation relative to its symmetric target. The Committee expects that economic conditions will evolve to warrant gradual increases in the policy rate, which is likely to remain below its longer-run equilibrium for some time. However, the actual path of the policy rate will depend on incoming economic data.

The Committee will continue its current practice of reinvesting principal payments from its holdings of agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities. The Committee expects to begin reducing its securities holdings this year, assuming the economy develops as anticipated. This program, detailed in the accompanying addendum, will gradually decrease the Federal Reserve's securities holdings by reducing reinvestments.

The Committee decided to maintain its policy interest rate at its current level.

Economic activity and employment have shown improvement, supported by widespread vaccinations and robust policy measures. However, sectors most impacted by the pandemic are still weak, despite some recovery. Inflation has increased, primarily due to temporary factors. Financial conditions remain supportive, partly due to policies aimed at bolstering the economy and credit availability for households and businesses.

The trajectory of the economy is heavily dependent on the virus's progression. While vaccination efforts are expected to lessen the public health crisis's economic impact, the outlook still faces risks.

The Committee is committed to achieving maximum employment and a 2 percent inflation rate over the long term. Given sustained inflation below this target, the Committee intends to allow inflation to moderately surpass 2 percent for a period, ensuring a 2 percent average over time and stable long-term inflation expectations. An accommodative monetary policy stance will be maintained until these objectives are met. The current policy rate will be held until labor market conditions align with maximum employment assessments, and inflation reaches 2 percent, on track to moderately exceed it for some time. Furthermore, the Federal Reserve will continue its monthly purchases of Treasury securities and agency mortgage-backed securities at their current pace until significant further progress toward maximum employment and price stability is achieved. These purchases contribute to smooth market operations and supportive financial conditions, facilitating credit flow to households and businesses.

The Committee will continue to evaluate incoming data for its implications on the economic outlook when determining the appropriate monetary policy stance. Should risks arise that could hinder the attainment of its goals, the Committee stands ready to adjust policy as needed. These evaluations will consider a broad range of information, including public health, labor market conditions, inflation pressures and expectations, and global financial developments.

The Committee decided to keep its policy interest rate unchanged. It also maintained its current approach to reinvesting principal from its asset holdings, continuing to reinvest payments from agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities.

Recent data indicate that economic expansion is proceeding at a moderate pace. Household spending and business fixed investment have shown moderate increases, and the housing sector has further strengthened, though net exports have been weak. The job market continued to improve, marked by solid job creation and a decrease in joblessness. Overall, labor market indicators suggest that the underutilization of labor resources has lessened since early this year. Inflation remains below the Committee's long-term goal, partly due to lower energy and non-energy import prices. Market-based inflation compensation measures declined, while survey-based long-term inflation expectations remained stable.

Global economic and financial developments could somewhat constrain economic activity and are likely to exert additional downward pressure on inflation in the near term. Nevertheless, the Committee expects that, with appropriate policy support, economic activity will expand moderately, and labor market indicators will continue to advance toward levels consistent with its dual mandate. The Committee still views the risks to the economic activity and labor market outlook as nearly balanced but is monitoring international developments. Inflation is expected to stay near its current low level in the near term but is projected to gradually increase toward 2 percent over the medium term as the labor market improves further and the temporary effects of lower energy and import prices fade. The Committee continues to closely monitor inflation trends.

The Committee anticipates that an increase in the policy rate will be appropriate once it observes further improvement in the labor market and is reasonably confident that inflation will return to its 2 percent objective over the medium term. This assessment will consider a broad range of information, including labor market conditions, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments. When the Committee begins to remove policy accommodation, it will adopt a balanced approach consistent with its long-run goals. The Committee currently expects that, even when employment and inflation are near mandate-consistent levels, economic conditions may, for some time, necessitate keeping the policy rate below levels considered normal in the long run.

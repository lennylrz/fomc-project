Today, the Committee increased its primary policy interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point. Concurrently, the Board of Governors unanimously endorsed a similar quarter-percentage-point rise in the discount rate, acting on proposals from the Federal Reserve Banks of Boston, New York, Philadelphia, Cleveland, Richmond, Atlanta, Chicago, St. Louis, Minneapolis, Dallas, and San Francisco.

The economy has shown considerable vigor this year. However, the Committee anticipates a deceleration to a more sustainable pace, influenced by a gradual cooling in the housing sector and the delayed impacts of higher interest rates and energy costs.

Regarding price stability, the recent surge in energy and commodity prices has had only a limited effect on underlying inflation. Productivity improvements continue to restrain the growth of labor costs per unit of output, and expectations for future inflation remain stable. Nevertheless, potential increases in the utilization of economic resources, coupled with elevated energy and commodity prices, could contribute to inflationary pressures.

The Committee believes that additional policy tightening might be necessary to mitigate inflation risks. The timing and magnitude of any such adjustments will be determined by how the economic outlook evolves based on new data. The Committee stands ready to adjust its approach as economic conditions change to achieve its objectives.

The Committee has decided to increase its policy interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point. This action reflects the Committee's assessment of current and anticipated economic conditions.

Since the [month] meeting, the job market has shown continued improvement, and overall economic activity has expanded at a moderate rate since the middle of the year. Employment gains have been consistent recently, and the jobless rate has fallen. Household spending has seen a moderate increase, though business fixed investment remains subdued. Inflation has picked up this year but is still below the Committee's 2 percent long-term target, partly due to earlier drops in energy and non-energy import prices. Market-based measures of inflation compensation have risen considerably but are still low; most survey-based measures of long-term inflation expectations have been largely stable in recent months.

Looking ahead, the Committee anticipates that, with gradual adjustments to monetary policy, economic activity will continue to grow moderately, and labor market conditions will strengthen further. Inflation is projected to reach 2 percent over the medium term as the temporary effects of past declines in energy and import prices fade and the labor market improves. The immediate risks to the economic outlook appear to be evenly balanced. The Committee will continue to closely monitor inflation indicators and global economic and financial developments.

The Committee expects that economic conditions will evolve in a way that justifies only gradual increases in the policy rate. The policy rate is likely to remain, for some time, below levels expected in the long run. However, the actual path of the policy rate will depend on the economic outlook as new data arrives. The Committee will assess realized and expected economic conditions against its goals of maximum employment and 2 percent inflation when determining the timing and size of future adjustments. This assessment will consider a wide range of information, including labor market conditions, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments. Given that inflation is currently below 2 percent, the Committee will carefully monitor progress toward its inflation goal.

The Committee will continue its current policy of reinvesting principal payments from its holdings of agency debt and agency mortgage-backed securities into agency mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing Treasury securities at auction. This is expected to continue until the normalization of the policy rate is well advanced, helping to maintain accommodative financial conditions.

The Committee decided to keep its policy interest rate unchanged.

The [event] pandemic continues to inflict substantial human and economic distress across the nation and globally. The pace of economic and employment recovery has slowed recently, particularly in sectors most impacted by the pandemic. Reduced demand and prior drops in oil prices have suppressed consumer price increases. Overall financial conditions remain supportive, partly due to policy actions aimed at bolstering the economy and credit availability for American households and businesses.

The economy's trajectory hinges significantly on the virus's progression, including vaccination efforts. The ongoing public health crisis continues to depress economic activity, employment, and price levels, and presents considerable dangers to the economic outlook.

The Committee aims for maximum employment and a 2 percent inflation rate over the long term. With inflation consistently below this long-run target, the Committee intends to achieve inflation moderately above 2 percent for a period, ensuring inflation averages 2 percent over time and long-term inflation expectations remain firmly anchored at 2 percent. The Committee anticipates maintaining an accommodative monetary policy stance until these objectives are met. It expects to keep the policy rate at its current level until labor market conditions align with its maximum employment assessments and inflation reaches 2 percent and is poised to moderately surpass 2 percent for some time. Furthermore, the Federal Reserve will continue to expand its holdings of Treasury securities by at least $80 billion monthly and agency mortgage-backed securities by at least $40 billion monthly until significant further progress toward its maximum employment and price stability goals is achieved. These asset acquisitions facilitate smooth market operations and supportive financial conditions, thereby aiding credit flow to households and businesses.

In evaluating the appropriate monetary policy stance, the Committee will continue to observe the implications of new information for the economic outlook. The Committee would be ready to modify its monetary policy stance as needed if risks arise that could hinder the achievement of its goals. Its evaluations will consider a broad spectrum of data, including public health metrics, labor market conditions, inflationary pressures and expectations, and financial and international developments.

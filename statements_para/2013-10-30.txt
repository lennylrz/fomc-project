The Committee decided to maintain its primary policy interest rate at its current setting. It also chose to continue its existing program of asset acquisitions, purchasing $45 billion in long-term government debt and $40 billion in mortgage-backed securities each month. The Committee will also persist in reinvesting principal payments from its holdings of agency debt and mortgage-backed securities into new mortgage-backed securities, and rolling over maturing government bonds.

Recent data since the [month] meeting indicate that economic expansion has proceeded at a moderate pace. The job market has shown some further strengthening, though the jobless rate remains elevated. Consumer spending and business investment have advanced, but the housing sector's recovery has slowed recently. Government fiscal policy is constraining economic growth. Inflation, excluding energy price volatility, has been below the Committee's long-term target, but long-term inflation expectations have remained stable.

The Committee believes that with appropriate policy support, economic growth will accelerate, and the unemployment rate will gradually fall towards levels consistent with its dual mandate of maximum employment and price stability. The risks to the economic and labor market outlook have, on balance, lessened since last autumn. While acknowledging that persistent inflation below its 2 percent target could pose risks, the Committee expects inflation to return to its objective over the medium term.

Considering the extent of government fiscal tightening over the past year, the Committee views the improvements in economic activity and the labor market since the start of its asset purchase program as reflecting growing underlying strength. However, the Committee opted to wait for more evidence of sustained progress before altering the pace of its purchases. These actions are intended to keep long-term interest rates low, support mortgage markets, and foster more accommodative financial conditions, thereby promoting a stronger recovery and ensuring inflation aligns with the Committee's dual mandate over time.

The Committee will closely monitor economic and financial developments and will continue its asset purchases and use other policy tools as needed until the labor market outlook has substantially improved within a context of price stability. In deciding when to adjust the pace of asset purchases, the Committee will assess whether incoming information supports its expectation of ongoing labor market improvement and inflation returning to its long-run objective. Asset purchases are not predetermined, and their pace will depend on the Committee's economic outlook and its assessment of the efficacy and costs of such purchases.

To support progress toward maximum employment and price stability, the Committee reaffirmed that a highly accommodative monetary policy will be appropriate for a considerable period after the asset purchase program concludes and the recovery strengthens. Specifically, the Committee anticipates that its exceptionally low policy rate will be appropriate as long as the unemployment rate stays above 6-1/2 percent, projected inflation one to two years ahead is no more than half a percentage point above the 2 percent long-run goal, and long-term inflation expectations remain stable. In determining how long to maintain this accommodative stance, the Committee will also consider other information, including additional labor market measures, inflation pressures and expectations, and financial developments. When the Committee decides to begin removing accommodation, it will take a balanced approach consistent with its long-run goals.

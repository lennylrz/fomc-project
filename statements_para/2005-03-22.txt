Today, the Committee increased its primary policy interest rate by a quarter of a percentage point.

The Committee assesses that, even following this adjustment, the monetary policy stance remains supportive and, alongside strong underlying productivity gains, continues to bolster economic activity. Economic output is clearly expanding at a healthy rate despite higher energy costs, and the employment situation is steadily getting better. While long-term inflation expectations are still well-anchored, inflationary pressures have intensified recently, and firms' ability to set prices is more apparent. However, the increase in energy prices has not significantly impacted core consumer prices.

The Committee judges that, with suitable monetary policy, the potential for both stronger and weaker outcomes for sustained growth and price stability should be balanced. Given that underlying inflation is anticipated to remain controlled, the Committee believes that policy accommodation can be withdrawn at a measured pace. Nevertheless, the Committee will adjust its approach as economic conditions evolve to meet its commitment to stable prices.

Separately, the Board of Governors unanimously endorsed a 25-basis-point increase in the discount rate. This action followed requests from the Boards of Directors of several Federal Reserve Banks.
